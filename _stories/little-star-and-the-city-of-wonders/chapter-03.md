Lusstoni took a deep breath and just froze with both his hands rested on the handle of his walking cane. The smell of the nearby meadow filled his nostrils. For a second he felt as good as he did back in the day, before his life went downhill. He almost forgot how it felt to be out in nature.

Omikraya frolicked around him, collecting flowers, which she later turned into a crown.

"Toni, look!" she shouted.

The man turned around and saw her pointing at the headwear she made, smiling so widely that he could see the gap between her teeth once more.

"Aren't you worried about the insects?" he asked.

"What insects?" she paused and looked at him.

"Ants, for example," he started. "Bees? Wasps? They could attack you if you wear flowers on your head."

The girl shook her head.

"They are cute, and they are my friends! Whee!" she responded, spreading her arms and resuming running around.

Lusstoni wished he could be that unbothered. In childhood he was terrified of insects.

"Let's go before your new dress turns green," he said.

"Toni, where are we going?" she asked and grabbed his hand.

He tried to release himself from her grasp, but the child had a surprisingly strong grip.

"I don't remember the proper name of this town, but I know they call it _Blue Pastures_," he said. "It's because large fields of blue flowers surround it, as you can see."

"You want to ask more questions?" she looked up at him.

Lusstoni nodded and pulled her towards the town.

This place looked better than the previous location they visited. The iconic white buildings with blue rooftops towered above the green fields. The town of Blue Pastures was built on a hill, so the view was excellent from every location within its border.

Lusstoni had a feeling that this was the day when he would find her family.

They roamed around the streets, meeting strangers and asking about Omikraya's family, but to no avail. That was until a kind-looking lady in advanced age stopped them and smiled in a friendly manner.

"I heard you are looking for her parents," she said. "Nice to meet you; I am her mother."

"Oh, how good to finally find you," he said happily. "Omikraya, go with your mother."

"She's not my mom," Omikraya said gloomily.

"Silly little girl, of course I am," the lady responded. "Thanks for bringing her to me."

She started dragging Omikraya away. The girl fought against her.

"I want to go with Toni; leave me be!" she kept shouting.

Lusstoni observed them from a distance. His excitement was really short-lived.  
All this time he thought she was behaving like this because she just wanted to stay on his airship, but all of the sudden he felt like she might have been telling him the truth.

The man scratched his chin.

"Didn't she say her mom _always_ calls her Little Star?" He finally said to himself. "Moreover, she said she was a nymph...not that I believed it, but this lady looks nothing like one. Not to mention, she seems too old to have a child so young..."

He suddenly started to panic.

"I turned her in to the wrong person!" he nervously said. "Something could happen to her. In that case my father would have said that it was my fault because I am stupid, and he would be right! I do want to get rid of her, but not this way. I got to do something."

Lusstoni followed them from afar. The stranger sat Omikraya in the garden in front of her little house. The girl seemed very unhappy about it.

"It's me again," she said as he entered the lot. "I just wanted to check up on Little Star."

The lady looked at him visibly confused.

"Who?" she finally asked.

"Your...daughter."

Omikraya wanted to protest, but the lady put a cookie in the girl's mouth to prevent her from speaking.

"She's fine, sir. Your job is done and you can go."

"Actually," he said. "I changed my mind, and I want her to leave with me."

Lusstoni put his hand in his pocket to find something that could help him disengage from this situation.

"Why? A daughter's place is on her mother's side, isn't it?" she asked.

The stranger paused suspiciously and said, "Let's talk about it over tea, shall we?"

The man agreed and watched the lady disappear into her kitchen. He could just grab Omikraya and run, but his mobility wasn't sufficient to create distance before she caught up with them. Lusstoni also didn't trust the tea she was brewing; it could contain anything.

Omikraya wanted to say something to him, but she gestured at her to be quiet. She observed him put some liquid on one of the garden chairs and quickly hide the container back in his pocket.

The strange lady went out with only two cups of tea and a teacup.

"I only brought some for you two," she said. "I am not in the mood for tea right now."

"Let me help you with this," Lusstoni said and took the items from her to place them on the garden table. "Maybe I jumped to conclusions too early, Madam; you seem to be so caring..."

"Thank you, dear; I knew you would come to your senses," she responded.

Lusstoni offered her a chair.

"Please, sit down; you must be tired," he said.

The lady took a seat and smiled, visibly pleased.

"I would like to hug the girl one last time before our ways split," he said. "We were together for so long; I will miss her."

"Go ahead, young man."

The stranger waved her hand at him and relaxed in the chair.

Lusstoni approached Omikraya, grabbed her arm, and said, "Now run as fast as you can."

They both rushed to the street.

The stranger tried to get up and stop them, but she couldn't leave her chair. She angrily yelled at the fleeing man.

"Cursed scoundrel from Norath!" she screamed.

"What did you put on her chair?" Omikraya asked as they ran down the street to escape the town.

"Super strong glue that I forgot to remove from my pocket," he said. "Don't look back. We got to make sure she won't catch up with us if she decides to undress to pursue us."

They started slowing down as Lusstoni's leg began giving him trouble. He noticed some people following them, so he threw a handful of short metal nails on the ground and forced himself to keep running until they were out of the town.

Lusstoni tried to catch his breath.

"I didn't think carrying all this junk with me could be useful someday," he said with an effort.

"I told you it was not my mom. Why don't you ever believe me?" Omikraya exclaimed.

"Didn't I go back for you?"

"After you let her take me!"

"But I did, so what's the problem?" Lusstoni asked and scratched his head.

"You don't listen to what I say!" Omikraya said, almost in tears.

"It's because you keep telling me nonsense about magic. No such thing exists."

"Of course it does!" she said. "How did you realize it was not my mom if you don't believe me?"

"You said she always calls you Little Star, and this woman didn't," Lusstoni responded. "I do listen to you."

Omikraya was too upset with him to continue the conversation, but it didn't stop her from grumpily following him back into his airship. She just crossed her arms and sat on the floor with her back towards him.

Lusstoni took a seat in the cockpit, raised the aircraft off the ground, and flew it straight ahead. He didn't know where to go next, but he was sure he didn't want to be there anymore.

The stress of the situation they just got out of still didn't seem to loosen its grip on him. He reached into his secret stash and fetched a bottle of alcohol from it.

_I know I shouldn't drink because I am piloting an aircraft and there is a child on board, but I will really not rest otherwise,_ he thought to himself.

He then opened the bottle and poured the liquid into his mouth. Some alcohol sprayed out of the bottle, staining half of his face. He wiped it with his hand.

_It's stronger than I remember,_ he said and closed the bottle and put it away.

Lusstoni took a deep breath and glanced through the window. He thought it would be easier to get rid of a child he didn't even know. The man felt strangely happy that he didn't have too many friends anymore. At least they couldn't see him now and imply he didn't really want to get rid of her.

He still didn't like Omikraya, but he wanted to become the person he should have been ages ago.

His brooding was interrupted by the very reason that had caused it.

"Toni, what is Norath?" she asked. Omikraya didn't seem to be upset with him anymore.

"Why do you ask?"

"It's because strangers keep using that word while talking to you, but I have never heard it," she said. "Is it a curse word?"

"No, it's..." He hesitated for a second. "It's the city I come from."

The girl looked at him with a surprised expression.

"How do they know you are from there?" she asked.

"It' because of my clothing," he responded and stretched in his chair. "People of Norath wear a different kind of fashion."

"But they always sound so upset about your city. Is it bad?" she asked. "Is this why you ran away?"

"I didn't run away from the city," he said. "That place is just different, and other people don't like it."

Omikraya looked at him, slightly confused. She still couldn't understand what was going on.

"So why did you leave?" she asked.

"It started with... no, no... wait," Lusstoni mumbled. "I was there, but it was her fault."

Lusstoni paused briefly.

"Then the accident and the hospital, but I thought it was..." he continued. "…but even after that I hoped it still wasn't over. She could still come back to me. I mean, she already married my brother, so she wouldn't even need to change her surname! We could still make it work."

"Lusstoni!" Omikraya shouted.

The man looked at her with a puzzled expression.

"I don't understand you," she said. "Tell me why you left; you're scaring me."

Lusstoni sighed.

"I had too many bad memories that were chasing me," he said. "I thought a little adventure would distract me in a good way."

"Did it?"

"It did distract me."

Sudden silence fell in the cockpit. They just stayed there without exchanging a word until Lusstoni said something again.

"Why did _you_ leave?"

"I had to."

"I am pretty sure your parents are worried about you," he said. "Tell me again where they are so that I can take you to them."

"They are on Crescent Moon Isle."

Lusstoni enabled the auto pilot and got up from his chair.

"But that place doesn't exist," he said and approached the map.

"It sure does!" Omikraya shouted.

"Then show me where it is on the map because I can't see it," Lusstoni raised his voice.

The girl took a crate and put it in front of the wall. She looked at the map and then pointed at an empty square in the sea.

"Here," Omikraya said.

"There is nothing there," he said, visibly irritated. "No island, no rocks, nothing beside dirty water and stinky seaweed. Tell me where you are really from!"

"It is here! It's just hard to see it from outside because of the core!"

"I got enough of this nonsense," he said. "I am about to show you that this is an empty point in the middle of nowhere. Then you will **have to** tell me where you came from."

Lusstoni returned to his seat and changed the course.

"We are very close to that place, actually," he exclaimed.

"No, I can't go back there!" Omikraya shouted frantically and pulled his sleeve. "My mom said I can't—"

"Shut up!" Lusstoni yelled and pulled his arm away from her. "I can't keep babysitting you forever; you aren't even my child!"

"But my mom said—"

"**Silence!**"

Omikraya began crying loudly, and Lusstoni wouldn't remain calm. Chaos ensued in the cockpit.

The man was so preoccupied that he didn't notice the zeppelin had begun descending and flying in a slightly different direction. It was as if it were sucked into an invisible vortex.

Lusstoni suddenly looked through the window and panicked. He began frantically pressing buttons and flipping switches, but nothing would stop the machine from going down.

"No, no, no!" he shouted. "What's going on?"

Dark clouds completely swallowed the aircraft. For a split second it seemed like it was the end of their journey.

The airship slowly fell onto the beach surrounded by big rocks. Had it fall a little to the right, it would have been completely destroyed, yet the aircraft seemed to remain in one piece.

Lusstoni checked the state of his vehicle, first inside, then outside. He desperately tried to find the reason why it landed this way. He was too busy to check where they were.

"I don't understand," he said as he scratched his goatee. "Everything seems in order, yet it won't fly. I don't know what caused it to behave this way."

"It's the core," Omikraya stated.

"The what?" he asked.

"The core of the star goddess," she repeated. "It creates a shield around the island."

"The...island..." Lusstoni mumbled and looked around.

They seemed to have landed under some sort of a cliff.

"The core is under the castle," the girl said and pointed up. "There."

Lusstoni looked upwards and saw the pinnacles of the towers.

"How can we get there?" he asked. He suddenly didn't feel so sure about his judgment. Could this be the island the girl told him about?

"I can lead you there," she said.

"Hold on. It's cold in here. Let me grab my poncho," Lusstoni said as he returned to the deck of his airship. He opened the big chest that stood in the main chamber and reclaimed some pieces of clothing from it.

"May I have a poncho too?" Omikraya asked.

Lusstoni sighed and rolled his eyes.

"You didn't feel cold until I pointed it out, did you?" he said and wrapped a thick piece of yellow cloth around her.

"It's my old scarf, but it's big enough for you to wear as a miniature poncho."

The girl clapped her hands.

"Now we look like twins!" she exclaimed.

"Yes, we are basically identical," he said ironically as he put on his yellow poncho.

Omikraya gestured for him to follow, and they walked across the beach.

The farther they got, the stranger the island looked to him. They climbed on some rocks with a big effort from Lusstoni's side. He saw large grasslands with sparse trees in front of him, although they did seem a bit more blue than what he had seen until now.

Strange little plants grew between the blades of the grass surrounding him. The view of a castle was clearer from here, but it still seemed to be quite a distance from them.

"Damn it," he said. "Do we _really_ need the core to get out of here?"

"My mom said that nothing would fly out of here for as long as it stays active," she said. "That's why she put me on a boat."

"Hmmm," Lusstoni grunted. "Then we got to find a way to deactivate it."

He approached a small lake nearby and knelt in front of it. He took off his top hat and goggles and threw some water on his face.

All of the sudden, a mysterious figure appeared above the lake.

"What have you done?" the stranger shouted.

Lusstoni looked up. It was an eerie woman with pale skin and purple hair, just like Omikraya's. She hovered above the water like a ghost.

"Why did you bring the princess here?" she asked with a harsh voice. "Her mother gave up everything to put her to safety. Take the child, leave this island, and never return!"

"I can't. My aircraft won't fly," he responded.

"Then you better find a way," she said. "The princess cannot stay here."

Lusstoni had some questions, but the mysterious woman disappeared.

"Was that..." he mumbled, "...was that a nymph?!"

Omikraya nodded.

"She is a friend of my mom."

The man got up and placed his top hat back on his head.

"I see," he said. Lusstoni felt like he should apologize to the girl, but he couldn't imagine being this vulnerable in front of a child. Treating her like an equal didn't seem right.

After a long while of lumping about, Lusstoni finally arrived at the gate of the castle's garden. It was open, but it looked like whoever lived there left it in a hurry.

Lusstoni looked around. He could see some distant villages and settlements below, but they also seemed abandoned.

They went through the ravaged garden and into the castle. The main corridor was covered in deep scratches, and damaged furniture was scattered across the hall. It resembled a scrapyard more than a royal structure.

"What happened here?" he asked as he kicked a broken plank away from the entrance.

"Father," she responded.

Lusstoni didn't understand, but he didn't say anything more. He felt ashamed of himself for treating her the way he did before he realized she was right all along.

They went up the stairs that had seen better days. Omikraya led Lusstoni into a large chamber that resembled a library.

"What is this place?" he asked.

"They keep history scrolls here," the girl said. "Maybe you can find something that would help you disable the core here."

Lusstoni approached the large book shelf in an attempt to find anything that would give him some context.

"I will look for my mom," Omikraya announced.

"Fine, I will stay here," he answered and began going through the endless scrolls and tomes.

The library chamber was only a little better than the corridor below, but at least the books survived whatever happened in that building.

Lusstoni found an old-looking scroll titled _The History of Crescent Moon Isle_. He unrolled it carefully not to damage the papyrus. He was surprised to see that it was written in the common language. He expected to see an enigma, but aside from the scribe's horrible handwriting, he didn't really see anything out of the ordinary.

_Ages after the fall of the Old Empire, humanity returned to worshiping the primal gods.  
It was the time of changes led by the noble Knight Callum.  
Devoted to Omikraya, the Blind Goddess of Stars, he led his people to victory and united the three kingdoms of men.  
The Goddess, pleased with her follower, rewarded him with a magical artifact, an island, and four of her maidens for him to choose one as a wife while the remaining three served him.  
The knight accepted her gifts, thus becoming King Callum the Devoted._

Lusstoni scratched his head.

"But what happened next?" he asked himself. "The scroll ends here."

The man searched through the bookshelf, attempting to find something more.

As he scoured, a notebook fell to the ground. Lusstoni opened it.

"Seems like King Callum wrote it himself," he said and began reading.

_The core is suspected to maintain divine magic on this island, yet it shields it from the outer world. None of our aerial inventions seem to work on Crescent Moon Isle, but in return the artifact offers us powers nobody can understand._

Lusstoni flipped the page.

_Our people can live forever in good health. All our problems can be solved by the magic of Goddess Omikraya. Praised be the core, praised be the power of the stars._

"This is all he wrote, strange."

Lusstoni put the notebook back on the shelf.

He noticed a barely visible vase full of scrolls in the corner. It looked like someone tried to hide them. But why?

Lusstoni approached it and gently removed one of the scrolls from it.

"This seems like a log of the royal scribe," he commented.

The man slowly opened the scrolls one by one.

_King Callum decided to destroy the history records of the times before we came to Crescent Moon Isle. He claims that all we need lies in the core. His decision caused riots._

_We, the people of Crescent Moon Isle, collectively noted the bad influence the core has on King Callum. His mind is progressively more and more disturbed, his judgment clouded._

_The six sons of King Callum decided to join the rebellion as education became forbidden. Maiden Ashidrella, the Queen, voiced her concerns._

"Oh, so Omikraya has brothers..." he said. "Perhaps I could find them and leave her with her biological family."

Lusstoni resumed reading the scrolls.

_King Callum seems to descend into madness. He began using the core to shift into monstrous forms in order to fight the uprising. The six princes died in the aftermath of the conflict. Their bodies were later found near the cliffs. All but one._

"There goes my hope."

_Queen Ashidrella announced her pregnancy. The kingdom seems torn between those who remain hopeful and those who fell into the pit of despair, as King Callum has forbidden the citizens from mentioning his deceased sons._

_King Callum named his daughter Omikraya in homage to the Goddess of Stars. Little hope that the kingdom had evaporated when he sealed himself in the vault beneath the castle to conduct experiments with the core._

"So the core is somewhere under this building," Lusstoni commented. "I should attempt to find the plans. Perhaps they would reveal the position of the vault."

The man looked into the vase and saw that there was one more scroll left in it.

_The kingdom fell as King Callum lost his sanity. Transformed into a beast, he began attacking the civilians. This time his alteration doesn't seem reversible. Queen Ashidrella placed her daughter and the remaining survivors on boats while she stayed behind to fight King Callum, allowing them to escape._

"This scroll is covered in blood. I don't think I will find the scribe who wrote those," Lusstoni said and placed the scripts back into the vase.

The man approached the desk that stood next to the bookshelf. He opened the drawer and saw the plans of the building.

"I got what I need," he said. "Time to find Omikraya. After reading these scrolls I really don't think she should be here alone."

Lusstoni began searching for her. He went chamber to chamber until he noticed a movement. He then stopped and looked through the partially open door.

Omikraya was sitting on the floor of what appeared to be a child's chamber.

The man entered the room. The walls were badly damaged, and her toys were scattered on the wooden panels.

"Was this your room?" he asked.

"Yes, I am looking for my favorite toy," she responded, "but they all seem destroyed."

Lusstoni helped her locate the least broken toy, which was a really difficult task.

"I didn't find my mom," she said.

The man felt like he had a big clump in his stomach. He suspected she died protecting the survivors, but he didn't know how to tell Omikraya.

"We can look for her a little later," he finally said.

The girl grabbed what seemed to be a plush bear and stood up.

"This one is not my favorite, but it will do," she said.

Lusstoni and Omikraya went to the top of one of the towers in the castle. The view from above was unlike anything he had ever seen. He knew it wasn't a healthy thought to have, but he wished he could show this land to Caermirella.

A rapid movement in the distance caught his eye.

"What was _that_?!" he asked and pointed at a strange creature roaming on the opposite side of the island.

The beast was long and covered with flowy dark fur. It resembled an overgrown ferret with long claws and a snout that looked like it had been crossed with an inbred cat.

Omikraya looked at him with a sad expression in her eyes.

"That...was my father."

Lusstoni didn't know how to react.

"Well, I can see the resemblance if I squint," he tried to joke.

"That wasn't funny," she said and lowered herself under the windowsill.

Lusstoni's eyes followed the beast from afar. It became apparent to him that if he wanted to leave this island alive, sooner or later he would have to face Omikraya's father. A part of him wished it wouldn't happen too quickly, but another knew that they don't have enough supplies to falter.

None of this was on his mind when he decided to go on an adventure.
