Standing among scattered tools and oily pieces of cloth, Lusstoni was making sure that his airship was ready to depart to Norath. He didn't expect to be this happy about going back home. Crescent Moon Isle gave him a real lesson about life.

It seemed that his vehicle suffered minimal damage and would fly without a single problem.

Satisfied with the results of the inspection, Lusstoni decided to reward himself with his last cigarette.

All of a sudden, he heard coughing, but this time it wasn't Omikraya.

"Oh come on," he complained. "Another one who can't be near me while I smoke?"

Ivi came out from behind the airship, pinching his nose with his fingers.

"Why do you do it anyway?" the hermit asked.

Lusstoni took a puff and exhaled the smoke through his nose.

"I never thought about that," he admitted. "I started very young, and I just kept doing it."

Ivi scanned the airship with his eyes as if he tried to memorize its appearance.

"Where is Omikraya?" he finally asked.

Lusstoni glanced at him indifferently.

"She's already inside," he answered and dropped the cigarette butt to the ground, then stepped on it, squishing it with his boot. "Give me a moment to clean this mess and we will depart if you're ready."

Ivi nodded and pointed to the bag he had over his shoulder.

"I think I already brought everything I need to survive. Would you mind giving me a double check?" the hermit asked.

"How am I supposed to know what you need?" the man answered with another question. "I am an aircraft pilot, not a survivalist. I can fly you wherever your heart desires, but knowing what you should prepare is not my area of expertise."

Lusstoni did know a thing or two, but he was never too confident about it. He would rather let people down than give them the wrong piece of advice.

Ivi seemed a little disappointed, but he didn't insist.

Lusstoni quickly collected his scattered things, then brought them on the deck. He carefully placed them in the corner of the main chamber, then wiped his hands with a silky handkerchief and headed toward the cockpit.

Ivi followed him along. He was impressed by Norathian technology.

Lusstoni took a seat in his pilot chair and looked at the hermit.

"So where do you want to go?" he asked and gestured at the map.

Ivi looked at the parchment for a moment, then pointed at a dark mountain pass to the southeast from Norath.

"I need to go here, between those spires," he finally said.

Lusstoni nodded silently, then pressed some buttons and turned a large knob. The airship began slowly lifting off the ground.

Ivi approached the window and watched the island getting smaller and smaller as they gained altitude.

"I never thought I would live to this day," he said.

"Me neither," Lusstoni responded. "I wonder if my radio works here."

He turned it on, and after a moment of noise coming out of it, they finally began hearing music.

"Incredible! It's like..."

"A bard?" Lusstoni asked and glanced at the hermit.

"How did you know I was about to say that?" Ivi asked with a surprise in his voice.

"Omikraya said the same thing."

Silence fell, and Ivi resumed looking through the window.

"Why are you going to these mountains anyway?" Lusstoni finally asked.

"There is something or someone I need to find," Ivi explained without looking at him. "Are you taking Omikraya with you?"

"Correct."

Ivi turned around and smiled.

"Brilliant!" he said. "One day I will return from my mission and visit you two in Norath. I will have to see how our little girl had grown!"

"Don't make this weird," Lusstoni answered. The wording Ivi used made him feel a little strange. "Listen, it will take us a while to reach your destination. Why don't you rest in any other room that isn't the cockpit? You can't even sit down here."

Ivi nodded with a defeated expression and left.

Lusstoni was alone once more. He made the music louder and put his goggles on.

The man looked at the world outside. The sun shone above him, and the sky was the most beautiful shade of blue. He felt like the world moved on without him while he was trapped on the island.

Lusstoni felt so insignificant in the grander scheme of things. He knew that the life of one regular person didn't have too much impact on the world as a whole, but he still hoped his would have more.

His brooding was interrupted by the sound of the door of the cockpit opening.

"Toni, do you have more cookies?" Omikraya asked as she walked in.

Lusstoni pretended that he was a little annoyed, although he appreciated a distraction from his thoughts.

"Yes, I have some in the hidden compartment under my bed," he responded. "Take them from there."

Omikraya blinked quickly.

"But how can I get them if it's hidden?"

"Then find it."

The girl went out of the cockpit, leaving him alone once more.

Lusstoni enabled the auto pilot and walked to the door, opening it just enough to see what was going on behind it. He saw Ivi sitting on the bench in the main chamber and Omikraya bringing him the can of cookies and her toy. They played together and shared food with each other.

The man tried to remember this moment. It was almost as if he had a family again.

Omikraya and Ivi didn't notice him. He went back to his chair and simply sat there while staring at the skyline.

Hours have passed, and he was still sitting there listening to his radio. Finally, he got up and walked into the main chamber.

"Ivi, we are nearly at the destination," he said.

The hermit's eyes shone with excitement. He ran toward the window and glanced at the gloomy tall mountains underneath.

"This place looks just as bad as I imagined it!" he shouted, then he looked at Lusstoni. "Remember what I said."

"As if I could forget," Lusstoni responded and theatrically rolled his eyes. "I will land the airship soon; be ready."

The man went back to the cockpit and began the landing procedure. After a moment he walked back to the main chamber and opened the door on the side of the deck.

Ivi enthusiastically hopped out of the vehicle and looked around.

"I think I will figure out the way from here," he shouted. "Until we meet again, handsome pilot!"

Omikraya looked at the departing hermit through the door, then glanced at Lusstoni and asked, "Why did he call you that?"

"I don't know," Lusstoni admitted. "Even I wouldn't go as far as using that word to describe me."

The two waved at Ivi and watched him disappear behind the dark rocks.

"This place seems dangerous," Omikraya noticed. "Do you think he will be alright?"

Lusstoni nodded.

"If he survived your father, he will survive anything," he said, clearly oblivious to the implication that he also made it out of the island alive.

He closed the door and headed to the cockpit.

Before lifting off, Lusstoni grabbed a bottle of water and took a big sip.

"It feels strange to drink something so plain," he said and grimaced. "Although I suppose I should get used to it."

Lusstoni put the bottle back in the bag next to his seat, and their journey resumed.

Omikraya knelt on the bench and pressed her face against one of the windows.

The sky was slowly turning gray, although it didn't seem that it would start to rain. She glanced below and saw a small village.

The sounds of Lusstoni's radio were filling the entire chamber, which made her wonder whether it was a good moment to go into the cockpit.

Omikraya approached the door and pushed it, opening it slightly with a low squeak. She squeezed her head through and saw Lusstoni sitting in his pilot chair as always.

The girl approached him and hesitantly pulled his sleeve.

"Toni?"

"Hm?"

"Are we going to Norath?" she asked.

"Yes."

"Where are we going to live?"

Lusstoni looked at her with an unimpressed stare.

"We are **not** going to live together," he said.

"Why not?"

"I told you I will take you to Norath," he responded. "I didn't tell you I would keep you."

Omikraya's face saddened.

"I can't live alone," she said.

Lusstoni shook his head.

"You will not live alone," he clarified. "I will find someone who will adopt you."

Omikraya's eyes filled with tears.

"Why do you want to get rid of me?" she yelled. "I thought we were friends!"

"Omikraya, you need a good and responsible person who will take care of you," he said calmly. "For now all you have is me."

"But that's the same thing!"

"I don't share your enthusiasm."

"Why don't you like me as much as I like you?" she shouted through tears. "You want to get back people who no longer care about you, but when I do, you want to get rid of me!"

"Calm down."

"I thought you would at least feel sorry for me because I don't have anyone else anymore!"

"Stop making it sound like you are the only one whose life is difficult," he shouted back. "Have you got any idea what I am going through, or are you just trying to push me to have it your way?"

"I don't want to talk to you ever again!" she yelled and left the cockpit.

Lusstoni tried to calm down. He didn't feel great about his reaction, but he really believed someone else should take care of her. By being nicer to her, he would never return to his old life.

The man grabbed the photo from his inner pocket.

"You would know what to do," he whispered.

Lusstoni got up to put the photo back on the wall where it belonged. He was about to hang it when he looked at the map. The little apple farm was close enough to pay them a visit on their way to Norath.

The man put the photo back in his hidden pocket and set the course to that location. He wanted to buy some fresh food. Perhaps Omikraya would like to return there? He didn't believe so, but he could hope.

Lusstoni didn't count the time. All he knew was that they arrived sooner than he thought they would. He landed the airship and walked into the main chamber.

"Omikraya, we are taking a stop here!" he shouted.

"I am not talking with you!" the girl shouted back from his bedroom.

"Except you are doing it right now," he responded. "Do whatever you want; I am going outside."

The man walked out of the airship.

One of the farmers found him before he even reached the establishment.

"I remember you!" the farmer said. "Dear gods, what happened to you?"

Lusstoni didn't realize how much staying on the island affected them both. He touched his face and realized his beard grew into a mess. His clothes were no better, and his top hat was the only thing that stopped birds from nesting in his hair.

"Do you remember the girl I thought was your daughter?" Lusstoni asked. "I tried to find her parents."

"Did you?"

"Yes," he said. "They both are dead."

"That's terrible," the farmer covered his mouth in shock. "Is she still with you?"

"Yes, she's on the airship."

The farmer glanced at Lusstoni, then at the airship. He grabbed his sides and said, "My family and I are about to have dinner. Why don't the two of you join us and tell us about your journey?"

"How much do I owe you for that?"

"Nothing," the farmer said and brushed his mustache. "We may be poor, but we would never let people in need starve. We share what we have."

Lusstoni began feeling terrible about himself. His stomach, however, had no care for his feelings. It let out a rumble so loud that it would put a lion to shame.

"I will call the girl for dinner," he finally said. He hesitated for a moment, then added, "Thank you."

Lusstoni went back to the airship, opened the door, and shouted, "Omikraya, we are at the apple farm, and we are invited for dinner. Come out if you want to eat."

"Fine, but I am still not talking with you!"

The girl grumpily followed him to the farmer's house.

The family brought some extra chairs for them and served some stew. It was nothing compared to Norathian cuisine, but Lusstoni really appreciated eating something warm at last.

He told the farmers about his journey; they could barely believe his words. As he was about to finish talking, he noticed that the eldest member of the family grabbed his stomach in pain.

"What's going on?" Lusstoni asked.

"Grandfather is ill," the farmer's wife explained.

Lusstoni never thought he would say such a thing, but all of a sudden he blurted out, "Is there anything I could do for him?"

He couldn't recognize himself. Who was this man in a top hat?

The grandfather shook his head.

"There is nothing that could help these old bones," he said. "I already made peace with death. I just wish I could go to Norath one last time to visit my wife's grave."

"Why is she buried there?" Lusstoni asked.

"She was once ill as I was," the grandfather explained. "The poor woman was too young, I will tell you that. The family paid a fortune to send her to the golden city for treatment. Our funds never recovered, and neither did she."

The old man coughed.

"We hoped they would return her body to us, but Norathians just buried her there. For ten years we had no way of entering the city, and now I am about to share her fate."

Lusstoni frowned.

"Your son said the dinner is free, but how about I take you to Norath as a payment?" he finally said. "I'm not sure if there is enough space in my airship, but I could take your entire family. Then you could return to the farm."

The grandfather's eyes brightened.

"Would you really do it, young man?" he asked. "That would mean the world for me. Perhaps people of Norath aren't all that bad."

Lusstoni nodded in silence and resumed eating. He felt strange being kind for once.

"Why aren't you saying anything?" the farmer asked Omikraya.

"I have nothing to say," she responded gloomily.

The family looked at Lusstoni, but he just shrugged. He would rather not explain why the girl was behaving this way.

Once the dinner was finally over, the farmers followed Lusstoni to his airship.

The deck of the vehicle was slightly too crowded, but luckily everyone fit inside. Lusstoni set the course to Norath, and the airship lifted off the ground with an effort.

The road to the city was incredibly short, but the family made it sound like it was the adventure of their lifetime. Lusstoni never realized how privileged he was to be born there. To him it was just some streets and a bunch of buildings, but to them it was a paradise beyond their reach.

Lusstoni saw Omikraya glancing through the window as he flew by the main gate of the city. She was trying not to show any emotions, but she was beyond excited.

The golden roofs reflected sunlight directly into the deck of Lusstoni's airship. All his passengers gasped in awe.

He had crossed this gate far too many times to feel any emotions.

The man landed his airship on the landing pad in the center of the city. The farmer's family sheepishly left the aircraft. Their clothes were too simple and ordinary to properly blend in with the crowd.

Lusstoni gestured ahead of him.

"I will lead you to the graveyard," he said. "It's not too far from here."

The group followed him like ducklings follow their mother. They gawked at every nook and cranny, nearly getting lost several times in the process.

Lusstoni was a tad annoyed, but he tried not to show it again. He felt like he had a second chance at being a decent person, and he didn't intend to lose it.

When they finally arrived at the graveyard, they could see that this part of the city was built just as over the top as the rest of it. Even Norathian plants were more nourished than they were.

Lusstoni approached the black iron gate and pushed it forward.

After some time spent searching, the grandfather finally found his wife's grave. He took his hat off and knelt in front of the cold, hard stone.

Lusstoni intended to leave them alone, but the farmer's wife grabbed his sleeve.

"Please, stay with us," she whispered.

The man sighed and stopped right next to her. He took a look at the grandfather.

The elderly man barely had any hair anymore. His head was round and shiny, covered in small brown spots. He brushed his long white beard and started at the gravestone in silence for several minutes.

The grandfather finally said, "Sarana, my beloved... after you were gone, I held on to this life as hard as I could to support those who still needed me. At first I fought to keep you by my side, then I fought to keep the family strong; however, in the life of each man must come a moment when he finally lets go. Until we meet again in the Eternal Gardens, my love."

The man leaned down and kissed the ground in front of the tombstone.

Omikraya approached him and put her little hand on his back. The grandfather wiped the tear from his eye and gave her a warm hug.

"Thank you, dear," he said as he put the hat back on his head. He then turned around and added, "I thank the both of you. I never would have gotten here on time without your airship. We are blessed to have you among us."

This feeling of belonging was so unknown to Lusstoni. After all these years of being treated like an outcast, he never even hoped to be a part of something one day.

"It was nothing, really," he said. "I have to go home for a while. Take your time to explore the city."

He gestured at the family and led them out of the cemetery.

"Do you see that long road over there?" he asked and pointed ahead. "If you follow it until the end and then turn left, you will arrive at a house on the hill. That's where I live. Go there and tell me when you need to return to your farm."

"We will," the farmer answered. "Thank you."

Lusstoni took Omikraya with him, and they walked across the street. The sound of his walking cane hitting the road echoed across the street.

The girl remained silent until she saw the house on the hill.

"Is this where you live?" she asked.

It was a short, simple-looking building. The white wooden fence separated the small garden on its side from the streets. The girl could see some of Lusstoni's failed projects scattered on the grass. The house seemed modest but cozy.

"Yes," he responded.

Lusstoni took the key out of his pocket and used it to unlock the front door. The entire place looked roughly as he left it, except it was significantly more dusty.

The man opened the window in the living room, left his walking cane by the wall, and then sat down on the couch.

"I am so tired," he said to himself.

Omikraya looked around the room and then took the seat next to him.

"I can't wait to sleep in a proper bed," he continued.

"Where am I going to sleep?" she asked.

Lusstoni stood up and walked toward the door on the side. He opened it and showed her the inside.

"This is my guest room," he said. "It can be your room, but it's nothing compared to the one you hand in the castle."

Omikraya glanced inside. The room was quite small, but it had a proper bed and furniture. She frowned.

"What is it?" Lusstoni asked. "You don't like it?"

"I do; I just can't be happy because I will only have it for a short time and then you will leave me."

He was trying to find the proper words, but a sudden knocking at the front door interrupted their conversation.

The man opened the front door. It was the family from the apple farm. They looked slightly unsure.

"Here you are; that means we didn't get lost, good," the farmer said. "We would like to return home now if that's not a problem."

"I will fly you there," Lusstoni answered. "Omikraya, I am leaving. Stay here; I will be back soon."

The door shut, and there was only her and the deafening silence. The girl decided to explore this new place. She opened his drawers and cabinets.

Small mechanical crabs swarmed the desk in one of his rooms. She pressed the button on one of the robots, and it began walking.

She played with them for a moment, but then her curiosity got the best of her, and she returned to rummaging through his things.

Omikraya opened the cabinet in the living room and saw a pile of paper covering something. She poked it with her finger and heard a metallic sound; shortly after, some of the sheets fell out of it, revealing a box.

The girl opened it. She expected a treasure, but it was full of old photos. She sat down on the floor and began browsing them.

"He was pretty," she said as she saw a photograph of Lusstoni as a child.

The next image depicted his family. She couldn't help but notice that his parents were sitting much closer to his brother than they did to him. There was so much sadness in his eyes even back then.

Most of his old photographs seemed miserable. She watched them one by one until she noticed that the last one was resting on top of scraps.

"Are these pictures as well?" she said to herself.

She began putting them together like a puzzle. Each photograph depicted him and Caermirella. These were the only photos of him smiling.

Omikraya wondered why he shattered them to pieces if she made him so happy.

Suddenly she heard his key unlocking the front door. She quickly threw the photographs back into the box and returned them to the cabinet.

Lusstoni entered the living room holding a bag in his hand.

"What are you doing?" he asked and lifted his eyebrows.

"Nothing," she answered and got up from the floor.

"Why is it so dark here?" he asked. "Ah, right. You don't know how to use the electricity."

Lusstoni turned the light on and handed her the bag.

"What is this?" she asked.

"A new dress for you."

"Why are you giving it to me?"

"You have to look good in the adoption center tomorrow."

"I hate you," she cried, dropped the bag, and went to the guest room to cry. The door slammed so hard it nearly fell out of the frame.

Lusstoni sighed and picked the dress from the ground. He noticed one of the scraps on the floor. He grabbed it and took a closer look. It was a piece of Caermirella's face.

The man opened the cabinet, and all its contents fell to the floor.

"Just like my life," he sighed.
