Lusstoni finally had a moment to sit down after the road to the castle and back made every fiber of his body scream in agony. He made them some tea on his portable burner and enjoyed it in front of his airship by the light of the lamp he put outside.

They had to make sure the beast roaming around the island wouldn't see their camp.

"Toni, I am hungry," Omikraya said as she sipped tea from a small cup.

"We could have some bread tonight and eggs for breakfast tomorrow."

"Why do you like eggs so much?" she asked.

Lusstoni shrugged.

"They are healthy and they contain all the nutrition you may need," he finally responded.

The man put the tea away and took the bread out of the bag he kept next to him. He gave Omikraya a piece and then he took one for himself.

He attempted to take a bite when Omikraya saw something shiny in his mouth.

"Toni, you have a golden tooth!" she said with excitement in her voice. "Were you born like that?"

"What?" he asked, visibly confused. "No, I lost that tooth some time ago, and a doctor replaced it."

Omikraya looked at him with wide eyes.

"You mean a healer?" she asked. "We don't have them here anymore. Father said the core will cure us so we don't need to learn medicine."

Lusstoni nodded silently.

"Which god is the patron of your city?" she asked.

"Huh? We don't have one," he responded. "People of Norath turned their backs on gods long ago to regain the control of their lives. We learned how to heal our wounds, produce food, fly, and create marvelous constructions and machines to ensure we had everything without having to beg for it."

Lusstoni paused to think about it.

"We have people who do science to further learn about the world around us," he finally added. "We are self-sufficient and have too many resources, which makes even the poorest ones seem like kings next to the people from outside Norath."

"What does the city look like?" Omikraya asked.

"It's suspended in the sky above a wasteland that used to be our home in the past," Lusstoni responded. "You can only enter it with an aircraft, which is the primary form of transport in Norath. There are tall golden buildings as far as you can see with beautiful man-made gardens in-between. In one of them there is a tall iron arch in the shape of a heart on which lovers can put a small lock as a symbol of their commitment. Caermirella wanted me to take her there once."

"Did you put a lock on the heart too?"

"No, I didn't bring one with me. She said she wanted to go there, not that she wanted to do it."

"I'm pretty sure she wanted to put one there too," Omikraya said.

"That would explain why she got angry at me later," Lusstoni commented with a hint of sadness in his voice.

"Your city sounds wonderful," the girl said. "I wish I could learn about the world without magic...can we go there?"

"Maybe one day," he responded. "If we leave this island alive, that is."

Omikraya looked at the ground.

"Yes, if Father lets us go..."

"You always call him 'father,' but I never heard you call him your dad," he said. "Is it because of what happened to him?"

Omikraya shook her head.

"Because he had always been my father, but he was never my dad," she said. "He never liked me, even before he turned into a creature. I like to learn, but he never wanted to teach me anything or listen to what I want to say. All he cared about was the core."

Lusstoni looked at the horizon. He never expected to relate to this child; he thought they had nothing in common.

"Now that you put it like this, I think I never called mine 'dad' either," he finally said.

"Is your father also a monster?" Omikraya asked.

"Not physically," Lusstoni answered, "but in a way he is."

The girl silently bit into her piece of bread.

"I have a task for you, Omikraya."

Lusstoni took out a small box from his bag.

The girl lifted her eyes, but she couldn't see any details in the dim light.

"Caermirella left this box on my airship about nine years ago. She asked me to open it and tell her what I thought about the contents, but I never managed to solve the puzzle that keeps it shut," Lusstoni said and handed her the object. "You seem like a smart child; maybe you could unlock it."

Omikraya looked at the box. The top part had some symbols engraved on them. She noticed she could move and reorder them.

The girl spent about twenty minutes shuffling the colorful tiles around until the box suddenly clicked.

"Incredible," he said. "It took me nine years, and you just unlock it like it's nothing. How did you know what to do?"

"Some of the patterns match the shapes I saw in the picture from your cockpit," she said. "The other ones look like the pins on your shoulder. I ordered them the same way as I remember them. Look, this one is like her earring, and this one is like her necklace!"

"I think I really need to start paying more attention," he admitted and took the box from Omikraya. His hand was shaking; he was not sure what awaited him inside.

Lusstoni opened the container and saw a piece of paper and a small brooch. He unfolded the letter and recognized Caermirella's handwriting.

_Dearest Lusstoni,_

_I've been thinking a lot about it lately, and I came to a conclusion: I would like us to move to the next step of our relationship. We could get married in Norath, then go for a journey into the unknown for one year so that you could see all these places you have always dreamed of._  
_Upon returning we could finally try for a baby. Despite everything, I still think you would be a great father! I know how your parents treat you and how difficult it is for you to change your mind. However, I want you to save this brooch and give it to our first child as a symbol of growing a new family. One where you belong._

_Love, Caermirella._

Lusstoni glanced at the brooch. It looked like a little golden tree with small crystals embedded at the crown.

He placed the contents of the box into his hidden pocket and put his hands on his face.

_So that's what she wanted_, he thought. _She never liked traveling, yet she planned a one-year trip for me. The family was never meant to be just for her; she wanted me to belong too. How could I treat her the way I did?_

"Toni?" Omikraya said with concern in her voice. "What happened? What was in the box?"

"Nothing," the man said, wiping his eyes. "It was just a note she left me."

"Do you need a hug?"

"No, no!" he said as Omikraya forced herself on him. "Let me go!"

"Don't be sad, Toni!" she said and patted him on the head. "I think we should go to sleep now so you can forget about it."

"_You_ can go to sleep. _I_ will stay here."

"Fine!" she said and began walking towards the door of the airship.

"Wait," Lusstoni said as he got up. "There is a bench in the main chamber hidden under a pile of misplaced things. You should sleep there; you will be more comfortable on it."

The man entered the room and started cleaning. In a short while Omikraya could finally see the furniture.

Lusstoni brought her one of his small pillows and an extra blanket from his dresser.

"Thank you!" she said. "But why do I get my own bed now?"

"Because I feel bad for making you sleep on my laundry," he said and placed the items on the bench.

Omikraya lay down in her new makeshift bed and covered herself with the blanket.

"Toni, can you read me a story?" she asked.

"Don't stretch it," he said coldly and left the deck.

Finally alone, Lusstoni sat on the boulder in front of the airship and grabbed a bottle of alcohol and a pack of cigarettes that he smuggled out of the cockpit when Omikraya wasn't looking. He took a deep breath. The air was cold and fresh.

_I've felt enough today,_ he thought, and lit a cigarette with a match. _Let's not do it again anytime soon._

Lusstoni was in the bedchamber attempting to button his shirt with only his right hand. The morning sun illuminated the room, which annoyed him slightly. How dare it shine when he didn't wish to see it?

A sudden knock on the door distracted him.

"Toni, may I come in?" Omikraya asked while trying to find any hole or creak that would allow her to see her friend.

"No, I am getting dressed," he said.

"Why were you undressed?"

"I took a bath when you were asleep. Leave me be."

"But why can't I be there with you when you are getting dressed?" Omikraya moaned unhappily.

"Firstly, because that's inappropriate," he said, "and secondly...I don't want you to be scared."

A silence fell and Omikraya began feeling uneasy.

"Why would I be scared?"

"Do you know that..." Lusstoni began, "the shiny gauntlet I am wearing isn't actually a glove?"

"What do you mean?" she asked. "What is it, then?"

"It's a replacement arm," he answered. "And I am not wearing it."

"I am not going to be scared. I can see you without it."

"Are you sure?" he asked.

"Yes! You can trust me!" the girl shouted excitedly.

Lusstoni sighed heavily and unlocked the door. He went out of the room and stood in the entrance with his partially lowered shirt showing the stump on his arm.

He glanced at Omikraya. The man could see that she was trying to be brave, but the sight was unsettling to her.

"At least you're not missing your right hand," she said, trying to find the silver lining.

Lusstoni rolled his eyes.

"I was left-handed," he finally said.

"Oh," she mumbled.

"I told you it was a bad idea," he commented, pulling the other half of his shirt back on his shoulder.

"No, I am not scared!" she shouted and hugged him, wrapping her chubby arms around his waist. "You are still Toni, my friend."

The man was floored. It wasn't every day that someone framed being himself as a good thing, especially upon seeing the parts of him that he was ashamed of.

"Omikraya, let me go; I've got to finish getting dressed," he said.

The girl released him and ran into the bedchamber. She opened the tall dresser that stood in the corner.

Omikraya pulled out a small colorful towel.

"It's pretty," she commented, folded it, and put it back in the dresser.

_Maybe she isn't so bad_, Lusstoni thought to himself.

"I will be in the main chamber studying the plans of the castle," he said. "Don't be loud."

The man went into the main chamber and sat on the bench where Omikraya slept last night. He put the plans on the small table in front of it and began trying to read the schematic.

"This doesn't make any sense," he muttered under his breath.

All of a sudden, Omikraya began singing.

"I told you to be quiet," he shouted.

"No, you told me not to be loud!" she answered. "I am doing what you asked me to do."

"Then stop singing," he said.

The deck became quiet for about one minute when a strange sound broke his focus.

"What are you doing out there?" he asked and stood up.

Omikraya was playing with her stuffed toy and doing something between whistling and rhythmical loud breathing.

"You told me not to sing, so I am pretending my bear sings!"

"Stop doing whatever this is!" he shouted and returned to the plans.

As soon as he sat down, Omikraya started thumping her feet and hands against the floor.

"What are you doing now?" he asked, more and more annoyed.

"The audience is clapping for us."

"That's it. Go outside and play there," he said and pulled her outdoors.

"But—"

"No," he said and closed the door.

Lusstoni resumed studying the plans, but no matter how many times he looked, he couldn't find any trace of the secret chamber. No hidden rooms, no veiled entrances, nothing.

The man put the plans down when a sudden thought appeared in his head.

"I sent her outside, but I don't know where her father is," he said to himself. "I must check if she's alright."

Lusstoni opened the door and glanced outside.

"Omikraya?"

Silence.

"Omikraya, where are you?"

The man walked around the airship, but he couldn't find her. He began suspecting the worst when he suddenly heard her voice behind him.

"Toni, I found some clay, and I am making a pot!"

He turned around and saw the girl with her outfit stained with mud top to bottom.

"Nooo, you destroyed your new dress!" he shouted. "Give it to me for washing before the stains solidify!"

Lusstoni angrily put some water from the lake into the big bowl he stored on the deck and began washing the dress with gray soap.

He looked around and saw the girl running across the beach in only her underwear.

"Omikraya! It's too cold for that; put something on immediately before you get sick!" he shouted.

She didn't listen to him, so he got up and forcefully put his coat on her.

"Stop being so incredibly stupid," he said. "I swear, fate has truly punished me with an annoying child. I can't even do any of the things I like because of you; I wish I never met you."

Omikraya started crying and ran towards the bushes on the other side of the beach.

Lusstoni returned to the bowl and resumed washing her dress angrily.

"Congratulations, you moron," he said to himself. "Now you have become the same kind of person as your father is."

The man sighed heavily and stood up, leaving the dress soaked in the water.

"Omikraya?" he shouted. "Where are you?"

He began searching for her. Once he picked up the sound of her sobbing in the nearby bushes, the task became easier.

"Omikraya, come back."

"No, you are being mean to me!" she shouted and resumed crying.

Lusstoni still felt ashamed for behaving like his parents would, so in the surge of newfound guilt, he decided to speak the way his father never would.

"I didn't mean it."

"Then why did you say it?" she asked. "Why would you call me stupid if you didn't believe it?"

Lusstoni glanced at her red and swollen face. She looked pitiful.

"Listen, I—" he said. 'I'm sorry. I just wanted to reunite you with your parents, but now the situation has gotten complicated, and I don't know what to do. I'm overwhelmed and I'm not even sure if we will make it out of this place in one piece. I never had to take care of anyone before. You know, when my little brother was born, my parents didn't even let me be anywhere near him. I wish I knew how to handle children."

"Why not?" Omikraya asked and wiped her tears.

"Huh?" Lusstoni was confused. "Which part are you responding to?"

"Why didn't they let you hold your little brother?"

"I don't know," he responded. "They said I would harm him, but I would never do that."

"Do you wish they let you hold him?"

"Honestly?" Lusstoni said, then ran his fingers through his goatee. "I think I would. Maybe then we wouldn't end up hating each other so much."

"I think they should let you do it."

"Probably." The man paused for a moment. "Are you still upset with me?"

The girl shook her head.

"No," she said. "You didn't mean it."

"Thank you."

"But promise not to call me stupid again," Omikraya demanded.

"I will try."

"Promise!"

"Alright…I promise."

"Toni, where are my clothes?"

"Damn it!" he shouted. "They are still in the water. I should have bought you more than one."

They looked around to check if the Cursed King was anywhere around them.

"Do you think we should go back to the castle and find you something?"

"If Father is not around..." she said.

They returned to the deck of Lusstoni's airship. He took his coat from her and replaced it with another one. It was an old cloak adorned with light brown animal fur.

"Toni, was it alive?" Omikraya asked, pointing at the collar.

"Yes, it used to be an animal," he responded as he put his coat on. "I think it was a weasel."

"Why would anyone hurt cute weasels to make ugly clothes out of them?"

"Their furs are comfortable," he said. "Why? Do you like weasels?"

"Yes," she responded excitedly. "But I prefer ferrets. They are so cute, and their eyes look like little black beads! Do you have ferret clothes?"

"I don't think I do."

"Good," she said under her breath, "because I wouldn't wear a ferret."

They left the airship and headed towards the castle. The air seemed warmer than the day before. It was _almost_ enjoyable.

Lusstoni had to rely on his walking cane a little more than he usually does. He was tired, probably because he couldn't rest well due to the recent events.

The two finally arrived at the gate of the castle. Lusstoni pulled it firmly, and it opened with a squeak. They carefully looked around, scanning the terrain for dangers. However, the area seemed clear.

They entered the building. It was just as abandoned as it was the last time they saw it. Who would have thought?

Lusstoni climbed the stairs with an enormous effort while Omikraya simply glided through them. She ran into her old bedroom and opened the half-destroyed wardrobe.

"Look, my clothes are still here!" she said. "And they are almost good!"

"Get dressed, then," Lusstoni answered and put his hand into his pocket. "I will go upstairs and spend some time in the tower."

Omikraya nodded and began picking her next outfit.

Lusstoni went to the upper level and sat down on the windowsill of the tower above. He sighed and lit a cigarette.

The man felt guilty for the situation they found themselves in. He knew that they wouldn't be stuck there if he listened to her from the beginning, but what was he supposed to do? Just leave her there?

Lusstoni shook his head.

He might have been a child-hating asshole, but he wasn't a monster.

He inhaled the smoke of the cigarette so hard that it made him cough.

"If we survive this island," he said to himself, "maybe I should take her to Norath. I could find someone to take care of her. I think it would be better than...this."

Lusstoni sighed and extinguished the cigarette against the windowsill. He jumped down and immediately regretted sitting there in the first place.

"Ouch, my damn leg," he complained. "Will there be no end to this?!"

"To what?" Omikraya asked, appearing out of nowhere. She was standing there wearing a light blue frilly dress and holding his old cloak in her small hands.

Lusstoni twitched and said, "You scared me. Don't do that!"

"Ew, you are stinky again," Omikraya pointed out.

"Yes, I am," he said. "I thought you already got used to that."

They walked out of the castle and headed towards their camp when a sudden noise drew their attention. They hid in the nearby bushes and saw the monster of Crescent Moon Isle sniffing for them.

Lusstoni and Omikraya held their breaths.

The beast caught a whiff of their scent and began coughing uncontrollably. Cursed King Callum ran away squeaking.

Lusstoni waited until they could no longer see him and said, "Is everyone in your family coughing from inhaling a little cigarette smoke? It's not _that_ bad..."

Omikraya looked at him and responded, "Smoking is not allowed here. Nobody ever does it."

"So I am technically an outlaw," he scoffed. "Good thing there is nobody to enforce the law…or a bad one, depending on how you see it."

"Toni, can we quickly go back before Father returns?"

"That sounds like a good plan," he agreed.

They rushed through the plains to reach the beach. Lusstoni couldn't help but think the beast was in the same place at the same time the day before. Perhaps if he observed the Cursed King from afar, he would find the hidden chamber.

There had to be a way. Tracking the best sounded like the only choice, but...could there be something or someone else possessing such information?
