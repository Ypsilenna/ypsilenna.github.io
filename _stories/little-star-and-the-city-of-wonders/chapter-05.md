Lusstoni still felt under the weather from the events of the previous day. Omikraya, however, wasn't burdened by any particularly heavy emotion. She was sitting next to Lusstoni as he studied the plans of the castle once more.

"Toni, your city sounds so interesting!" she said. "I think it should be beautiful too."

"It is," he cut short without lifting his eyes off the paper.

"I think the people there should be smart."

"Mhm."

Omikraya looked up at him, clearly missing the hints he was giving her.

"Maybe if I went there I could become smart too..." she said with hope in her voice.

Lusstoni glanced at her briefly and resumed studying the plans.

"Maybe," he responded without enthusiasm.

"Could we go there together?"

The man sighed heavily and put the plans back on the table.

"I don't know that yet," he said. "Wait until I figure out how to get off this island. I can't do it if you keep interrupting."

"Toni, I—"

"Omikraya, stop asking me about Norath and let me focus, or else we will never go anywhere!"

The girl tangled her fingers shyly.

"But this time it's not about that," she said.

"What is it, then?" he asked and lifted his eyebrows.

"I'm hungry," she responded.

"I think we still have some eggs."

"I don't want eggs; we've been eating them for days!" she shouted in an upset manner.

Lusstoni got up and opened his food stash. It was a crate made of dark wood that had seen better days, but it was still good enough for him.

"I got some food in cans here."

"What are cans?" she asked curiously and glanced into the container.

Lusstoni looked at her with confusion in his eyes.

"Huh? You don't have cans here?" he said and pulled one out.

"A can is a metal container in which you can store food," he resumed. "This one contains beans in tomato sauce."

The girl grimaced.

"Eww, beans!" she shouted.

"This one has olives, and this one has pickled paprika," he said as he showed her the cans one by one.

"What's in that jar?"

The girl pointed at the glass container in the corner of the crate.

"Raisins. Do you want some?"

"No! Toni, why don't you have any tasty food?"

Omikraya stomped angrily.

"I think it's tasty," he responded. "Feel free to find your own food if you don't like mine."

Lusstoni put the cans back into the crate and closed the lid.

"Alright, let's go!" she shouted excitedly.

"Wait, where do you think you are going?"

"We will find some food, just as you said."

Lusstoni got up and grabbed his walking cane.

"As long as you don't mind meeting your daddy..."

Omikraya paused her happy chatter. The look of disappointment showed briefly on her face, but it finally had to give way to her renewed excitement.

"I don't think we will meet him!" she exclaimed.

"I appreciate your optimism," Lusstoni said sarcastically and put his yellow poncho on. "Lead the way; I want to see you find something better outside."

The girl grabbed his hand firmly and led him outdoors.

The day was as pleasant as a spiceless porridge. Gray clouds obstructed the sky, and the air was suspiciously humid.

They stood on the beach, and she pointed her chubby finger over yonder.

Lusstoni glanced at the murky gray saltwater. It didn't seem particularly inviting.

"We can fish!" she finally said.

"Great!" he responded with a fake enthusiasm. "Have you ever done it?"

"No..." she admitted. "...but it should be easy!"

"No fish for you, then, because I have never done it and I am not eager to learn now."

"Alright," she said. "Another idea, come!"

The girl happily skipped across the entire beach and climbed on top of the rocks. She put her hands in front of her eyes as if she held binoculars until she noticed something and pointed at it.

"I don't know what you are showing me," Lusstoni said.

Omikraya went closer to the plant she saw.

"Berries!" she exclaimed and ate one without thinking.

Lusstoni carefully observed her for several minutes without saying a word. He finally grabbed one, rubbed it against his clothes, and tried it.

"You ate one!" the girl pointed out excitedly. "Do you trust my foraging skills?"

"No," he said. "I only did it because you also ate one of these berries and didn't die."

"But you are supposed to take care of me!" she complained. "Aren't you worried something would happen to me?"

"Correction: I am not supposed to do anything," he pointed out. "The only reason I let you hang around is that I feel sorry for you."

"That's not true," she said. "It's because you like me!"

Lusstoni sighed.

"Whatever," he finally said. "The berries are tasty, but it's not enough for a full meal. We got to find something else."

"Let's go there!" she grabbed his hand once more and pulled him toward the settlement situated on the western side of the island. "There should be some food left in that village!"

Lusstoni silently followed her. He was too exhausted to argue with her.

The road was shorter than he expected it would be.

The man looked around. Some of the wooden houses appeared burnt, whereas others were simply slightly moldy.

"Delightful," he commented sarcastically.

Lusstoni was about to ask her to leave when something grabbed his attention. It was a statue right in the middle of the settlement. He approached it.

The sculpture was made of some sort of white stone. Whatever happened in that village didn't seem to have an effect on it.

The man looked at the details of the sculpture. It depicted a blindfolded woman chained to a group of stars with eyes on them. He was not sure whether the look on her face was serene or disturbed.

"What is this?" he asked.

"This is a statue of the goddess Omikraya," the girl said. "My mom said she looks like this because she was born blind and she used her magic to connect to stars so that she could see us from above."

Lusstoni touched the statue. The stone felt incredibly smooth under his fingers. However, the longer his hand stayed on it, the more he felt he could hear incomprehensible whispers coming from the back of his head. The man twisted his lips.

_I must be tired_, he thought to himself and withdrew his hand.

"Do you really think we can find some food here?" he asked.

"It must be here somewhere," she responded. "People just left everything when we had to get in the boats."

"Why were you alone when I found you?"

Omikraya looked at Lusstoni with a hint of sadness on her face.

"There was a storm when we arrived, and I got lost," she said. "I don't know where their boats went."

Lusstoni nodded. He didn't realize how much this child had to go through until now. His own problems seemed small in comparison, and she was still so young.

"Let's look for something to eat," he said and looked away.

They started searching the huts. There was nothing of value in the first two.

The man approached the third one. The door seemed to be covered with seaweed, which was rather strange, but he decided not to question anything on this island anymore.

He pushed the gray wooden door, and it opened with a loud squeak. The inside was quite dark, and the air smelled like fish and salt.

Lusstoni squinted. He noticed a crate on the ground, although he wasn't sure what was in it.

The man knelt in front of it to investigate. Omikraya silently observed him until a sudden noise sent shivers down their spine.

"So the Princess is alive..."

The voice sounded like neither a man nor a woman, but it certainly was human.

Lusstoni turned around quickly, trying to see where the sound came from. He saw a mysterious figure sitting in the dark corner by the table, observing them carefully with cold gray eyes.

"Who are you?" Lusstoni asked and took a defensive stance.

The stranger approached him with their hands in the air.

"I'm not going to harm you," the mysterious person stated and took their hood off. "You can call me Ivi."

Now that the stranger was closer to the light, Lusstoni could see that their face resembled the one of the lady above the lake, but their eyebrows and medium-length hair seemed to be dyed black.

"Your name doesn't answer my question," he said.

Ivi went closer to the open door. The light from outside shone on the stranger, revealing their clothes. It was no wonder Lusstoni didn't notice somebody was there. Ivi's entire outfit was almost black. However, he still couldn't tell who exactly was standing before him.

"Oh, I'm just a hermit," the stranger said. "I'm just...surviving here."

"Then why didn't you get on the boats when the island was being evacuated?" Lusstoni asked and raised his eyebrows slightly.

"You know the history of our island, splendid!" Ivi clapped both their hands. "They didn't take me with them because they were not aware that I live here."

There was something slightly sultry and magnetic in this stranger's voice, which gave Lusstoni a new range of conflicting feelings he never thought he could have.

"How is that possible?" he asked. "This island is quite small."

"Let's say I had to hide myself from the king and I was too good at it," Ivi responded. "But enough about me; tell me about you. Who are you, and how did you find her?"

The stranger pointed at Omikraya, which made her feel a little insecure.

Lusstoni rolled his eyes.

"How did I find her? Against my will," he said bitterly. "I'm Lusstoni of Norath City."

Ivi rubbed their chin.

"Norath, hmmm? I don't know where that is."

"I'm not surprised," Lusstoni said after a brief moment of silence.

"Mister Ivi, do you have food?" Omikraya asked and pulled their sleeve.

"Of course! It's over there."

Ivi approached an old crate and opened it with a rusty knife.

Omikraya took a look inside. The entire container was full of large, smelly fish covered in such a high amount of salt that it could be used as gravel on the village roads.

"Toni, I will have beans," the girl said. Her face turned green.

"They only look so bad because they are raw," Ivi said. "They are quite tasty when you remove their skins. Here, try it!"

The hermit attempted to give Omikraya their knife, but Lusstoni intercepted the item angrily.

"Have you lost your mind?" he almost shouted. "I didn't protect her all the way until here just to have it undone by sharp objects."

Ivi was initially confused, but suddenly they understood the issue.

"Oh, right, I'm sorry," they said. "I've been isolated for so long that now I don't know what is acceptable. Although, I suppose you did not come here to socialize with me."

Lusstoni put the rusty knife on the table and leaned on his walking cane.

"That's right, we did not," he said. "In fact, all I want to do is to figure out how to get under the castle and disable the core so that I won't have to look at this island ever again."

"I understand," Ivi said and nodded. "We share a common purpose, then. I know how to get there, but I need help disabling the core itself."

"Continue."

Ivi approached one of the shelves in the hut and grabbed a pile of papers. After a full minute of digging through them frantically, the hermit finally found the correct one.

"Look at this," Ivi said. "I observed the Cursed King for many days and nights and noticed something peculiar. It seems like the core controls him to some extent and makes him walk the same path every single day. Twice per day he manages to snap out of it. That's usually when he tries to track me."

"Why?" Lusstoni asked, slightly confused.

"Because I rejected the core," Ivi answered. "He wants to use it to turn everyone into a beast, just like himself. Take a look at this map I drew."

The hermit put the paper sheet on the floor. The light coming from outside was shining right on it. The mysterious person then pointed their finger at the sketch resembling the castle.

"The Cursed King always appears in the morning with the rising sun," Ivi said. "He walks down the main road into this village, then he heads towards this one to the north."

Lusstoni observed the sketch in total silence. He had just realized that the only thing that protected him from the beast seemed to be the cliff they were lucky to land next to.

"He then goes to the western beach and walks all the way down to the southern village," Ivi continued. "After going there, he heads back to the main road and disappears under the castle."

The hermit rolled the piece of paper like a scroll.

"There is a crevice hidden behind bushes on the right side of the castle," Ivi said. "That must be where the hidden chamber is. It's not visible on any of the castle plans because it was a natural formation."

"That explains it," Lusstoni sighed. "Let's say I find the entrance; how would I enter?"

"I had a problem with it too," Ivi admitted. "Turns out each one of King's closest servants had some sort of a rune attuned to the hidden door. Look around the castle and maybe you will find one."

"If you know where it is and how to gain access, why didn't you already do it?" Lusstoni asked, slightly irritated.

The hermit cleared their throat.

"There are two significant issues," they said. "Firstly, you have to make sure you get enough time to go in and out before the Cursed King returns. Secondly, you have to deactivate the core upon entering to disable the protective barrier above the island and weaken the king. It's a little...problematic, to say the least."

"How so?" Lusstoni asked.

"You see, many tried to do it before us," Ivi explained. "They tried to make a tool to destroy the artifact permanently, but the problem is that the moment the core touches your skin, it changes something in you. Whoever attempted to destroy it was altered by its magic and stopped desiring its demise."

Lusstoni grimaced. He had the feeling that getting out of this place was close to impossible. He really thought the person in front of him had a good plan. He didn't want to stay there forever.

"So...what are we supposed to do?" he asked.

"We could transfer the power of the core to another vessel," Ivi answered. "There are purple crystals growing out of the ground like plants. Seek the biggest one you can find, then get a tool that would allow you to harvest it."

"Why would I do that?"

"You could direct it at the artifact, and its power will start flowing towards the crystal, effectively weakening the core," Ivi said. "You will just have to remember to hold it through something to avoid contacting the flowing magic."

"Wait, wait, wait," Lusstoni stopped the hermit. "You want me to go there and risk my life and sanity like that, but... are you even sure it works? Where are you in this plan?"

Ivi looked at him slightly bewildered. They imagined Lusstoni's reaction would be somewhat more positive.

"I will try to keep the king away from you while you do that," the hermit said. "And I think it will work because it was described in this scroll I stole from the castle before all old knowledge had to be destroyed."

Ivi opened a dresser that stood on the left side of the room and reclaimed something big out of it. It was a large scroll that looked almost the same as the ones Lusstoni read in the castle.

Omikraya ran towards them and looked at the parchment. Lusstoni grabbed it before she managed to make it dirty with her chubby little fingers. The man took a quick glance inside.

"Correct, it really is described here," he said and paused for a moment. "But I still don't trust you."

"Come on, I am a man of my word!" Ivi exclaimed. "I know what you are thinking: 'Can I trust this gentleman? He is so mysterious...' and I say, 'Of course you can!'"

"Yes, that was... _exactly_ what I was thinking," Lusstoni commented. "Why are you helping us? What's in it for you?"

"I want to leave this island just as much as you do," Ivi said. "With all the boats gone, your flying machine is my only hope."

Lusstoni slightly opened his mouth in shock. He was sure he didn't mention it in front of this person.

"How did you know it could fly?"

"I saw it fall from the sky several days ago." Ivi paused for a moment. "Not to mention, I have some unresolved...issues with the king. Weakening the core should revert him back to his original form. That's when he can answer for his crimes."

Suddenly a strange noise filled the air. It was like scratching combined with very quiet whispers.

"Shh, can you hear that?" Ivi asked.

"Yes, what is it?"

"The Cursed King is here; he must have broken free again, and he's looking for me," the hermit explained. "Quickly, take the princess and leave through the small passage that goes to the beach."

"I could help you fight him," Lusstoni said, but he didn't even know why he offered his aid. After all, he didn't even know how to combat a large dog, let alone a cursed magic beast.

Ivi shook his head.

"If you manage to catch a glimpse of him, each red stripe on his body is a wound I inflicted on him. Every night he returns to the core, which heals him. He can only be delayed, and that's something I can handle on my own. Now go."

Lusstoni grabbed Omikraya, and they sneaked out of the hut and headed towards the beach. The last thing he saw was Ivi bravely fighting off the beast with a rusty sword.

The hermit wasn't lying; the Cursed King's body was covered with glowing red marks.

Before they returned to the camp, Lusstoni decided to benefit from the distraction in the ruined village and quietly approached the area around the castle. He walked with his good hand touching the outer wall of the fence until he felt something under his fingers.

"This rock feels natural; this must be the correct place," he said to himself.

The man found a small road between the bushes and arrived at the crevice. He looked at the wall. The rocks were blending in with a material he found familiar.

"Can't be!" he said.

"What?" Omikraya asked.

"The entrance to the secret chamber is made of regular human metal," he said. "I don't think I will need any magical rune to enter inside; I can just bring the new metal cutter I bought a while ago and could never use. This is the best day of my life!"

"Why does that make you happy?" Omikraya asked, visibly surprised.

"It's a man thing; you will not get it," he answered. "Let's go before your pappy comes back and makes a blood sausage out of our intestines."

"What's a blood sausage?"

"Something I will feed you in Norath and something you will never want to eat again, I am sure."

They ran back as quickly as Lusstoni's leg allowed them to. Lucky them, the king was still too busy to acknowledge them.



The sun was setting when they finally arrived at the airship. Lusstoni gave Omikraya some beans before sending her to sleep. He was surprised she didn't complain. Poor thing must have really been scarred from seeing Ivi's fish stash.

Lusstoni waited for her to go to bed, and then he locked himself in the bedchamber. He intended to drink and smoke as he always does, but he suddenly realized he almost ran out of his stock.

_Damn it_, he thought. _We really must leave this island; I don't think I can survive here for long without my favorite things._

The man took his coat off and lied down on his bed. He pulled out the photo hidden in his inner pocket and stared at it for a while.

"Caermirella, you won't believe the story I will tell you once I finally meet you in Norath," he said quietly. "I'm pretty sure you will like the girl too. She can be a little demon sometimes, but she's not a bad kid."

Lusstoni sighed and placed the picture on his nightstand.

"I hope Ivi is doing alright," he said to himself as his mind slowly descended into the realm of dreams.
