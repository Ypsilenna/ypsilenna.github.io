For a second all his worries were gone. There was only her, young and beautiful, like a jewel under the crown of the ballroom. She stood in front of him in a red sequin gown. Caermirella shone like a star. Everyone's eyes were on her, and for a good reason.

She reached for his hand. Lusstoni eagerly tried to grab it. He knew this day could be his last chance to make things right. Their fingers almost touched when an ear-piercing noise abruptly shattered the illusion.

The man woke up, and he had made some mistakes. Not now necessarily, but in his entire life in general.

"What do you want?" He growled with a raspy voice, still dizzy from his sleep.

"Toni, I am hungry!" Omikraya squeaked as she jumped on his bed.

"I didn't let you give me a moniker," he mumbled as he wiped his eyes. "Why didn't you just grab something from my stash rather than further destroying my good time on this godforsaken planet?"

"I want you to make me a breakfast because I don't want to mess with your things!" The girl said with a tone that probably was supposed to sound all grown up.

"Why are you so respectful all of a sudden?" The man knew something was amiss when he heard it.

Omikraya smiled widely, revealing a missing upper tooth.

"I've always been that!" She lied.

Lusstoni dragged himself out of the bed and went into his kitchen corner. He rummaged through his things for a moment for a portable burner.

The man walked out of his airship and placed the item outside. He then went back in and shortly returned with a frying pan.

"You will have eggs," he announced.

"But I want—"

"That wasn't a question," he interrupted her.

"Eggs are boring," she said and crossed her arms.

"I don't care," he responded as he heated the pan. "I am doing you a favor; if you don't like it, you can just leave me alone. I would greatly appreciate that."

"I will have eggs," she said.

"Didn't you just say they were boring?" He asked, raising his eyebrows up.

"I will eat them to stay with you!"

Lusstoni sighed heavily. He felt like he was making her feel welcome, which wasn't his intention.

Omikraya's stomach rumbled.

The man put one fried egg on an old-looking plate and handed it to the girl along with a fork that was, simply put, clean enough.

She sat on the ground and began eating.

"Toni, have you got something to write?" She asked.

"Huh? No, why?"

Omikraya looked at him with a funny expression.

"I could draw a line between the two dots on your cheek and make it look like a smile!" She exclaimed happily.

"There will be no smile on my face; now eat up and don't annoy me!" He burst out.

Omikraya resumed her breakfast.

Lusstoni walked away from her to smoke in peace. The man took a drag on his cigarette and put his right hand on his cheek. He had always been conscious about his beauty marks. The child didn't seem to judge his appearance, but it still stung him when someone brought it up.

He stayed there for several minutes until he finally extinguished his cigarette and returned to the burner.

"You stink now," Omikraya commented.

"I know," he responded in a soulless manner. He heard that line too many times in his life, but he never had the will to do anything about his habits.

Lusstoni sat on a boulder next to her and looked ahead with a blank stare. He still couldn't believe what he got himself into.

He finally got up, collected his things, and put them on the deck of his airship.

Omikraya followed him happily, bringing her plate with her.

The man sat in the cockpit and looked at the map. In his head he was already brewing a plan.

There was a small town nearby. He could start his search there. Somebody must have known the girl. Perhaps her parents were looking for her, and they would reward him handsomely for bringing her back.

Lusstoni got up and walked towards the map, making circles around the locations with the mechanical fingers of his prosthetic hand.

He nodded to himself and left the cockpit to look for the girl.

Omikraya was sitting down on the floor and polishing her plate with a piece of cloth.

"What are you doing?" He asked.

"Cleaning!" She said excitedly.

"You should have just put it on the pile," he told her. "I would have gotten to it eventually."

"The... pile?"

"Nevermind," he said under his breath. "Listen, we will now go to the nearest town. I want to ask around if anyone knows your parents."

"They are not there," she exclaimed.

"So where are they according to you?"

"On the island," she insisted, "and my father is scary, so everyone left."

Lusstoni didn't believe her. He was sure she was just messing with him so that she could stay.

"What are their names?" He finally asked.

"My father is named Callum," she responded.

He nodded.

"That sounds plausible..." he said. "What about your mother?"

"Her name is Ashidrella, and she isn't scary!"

"That's not even a human name!" He exclaimed.

"She isn't human," she said. "She's a nymph! I am half-nymph!"

Lusstoni raised his face into the sky and growled.

"Again with your fantasy?! Just tell me where your parents are."

"On Crescent Moon Isle," she responded as if it were obvious.

"How would they even get there?" Lusstoni asked with frustration in his voice.

"My father was a knight, and he got the island from the Star Goddess."

"Huh?" He couldn't believe his ears. "But that would make your dad about five hundred years old. There are no knights anymore."

Lusstoni shook his head and returned to his cockpit. He then set the course toward the town he had picked. Omikraya followed him joyfully, utterly ignoring his outbursts.

Lusstoni was sitting on his pilot chair and holding the steering wheel firmly. His eyes were dead set on the horizon.

"Toni, how does it fly?" She finally asked.

"Gas," he forced himself to answer her question.

"Oh, like the one that comes out of—"

"No," he interrupted her.

"Why are you wearing goggles?"

"So that the sun won't burn my eyes out," he responded.

"What is this thing?" She pointed at the object with buttons next to him.

"That's a radio," he explained. "It plays music."

"Oooh, like a bard?"

Lusstoni just quietly turned it on. The music filled the entire cockpit. He hoped that now she would leave him be.

It worked for a moment. Omikraya tried to ask him more questions, but he was pretending not to hear her over the sounds of the radio, so she finally gave up and left the cockpit. He was finally alone, but for the first time in eight years he didn't actually feel at home in the silence.

The airship hovered silently above the green hills. The area below wasn't a wasteland, but to Lusstoni it almost looked like one. However, any land not covered with tall golden buildings would make a Norathian feel this way.

They approached the nearest town. It was the kind of settlement that made the visitors wonder whether they would lose their money or body parts, but Lusstoni was desperate enough to find Omikraya's parents that he decided to go there one way or another.

The man landed his airship on the rocks near the entrance and walked out of the vehicle. They stood in front of a crude wooden arch with _the_ _Town of Perkahn_ engraved on top. The place didn't seem welcoming in the slightest.

They walked down the road leading into the center of the town. The stone that paved it was corroded, almost as if nobody cared to make it functional. Omikraya observed the dirty walls of the buildings they passed and grabbed Lusstoni's hand out of fear. The gesture frustrated him, but he said nothing. He had to figure out the way, and it was better for her to stick close until he found a good opportunity to drop her somewhere safe.

Truth be told, it probably wouldn't be here, but he had to make sure.

Lusstoni stopped to look around. This town was built in a strange and chaotic way. He realized that he would need to ask somebody to find his way around the city. But what was he looking for? An orphanage? The town's leader? Maybe their sheriff or whoever was responsible for maintaining order in that hole? The man wasn't sure, and he couldn't focus because his bloodstream had dangerously low alcohol content. He was not used to thinking in such circumstances.

A bar caught his attention. It's the kind of place where people trade their secrets like they're a commodity; perhaps he could find someone who knew Omikraya. That, and he could finally get something to drink. Staying dry for so long should be a crime.

They walked through the filthy door into a room that didn't look much better than the rest of the town.

There was a rusted jukebox playing strangely sorrowful music in the corner. It wasn't possible to change it, however, because it was surrounded by thugs who side-eyed the man as he entered.

Lusstoni approached the bartender. The man behind the counter had the posture of a large sack of potatoes if one could also put a scar on it. His oily black hair was tied on the back of his head with a piece of gray linen string.

The bartender glanced at them suspiciously and asked, "Why did you dye your daughter's hair purple?"

Lusstoni exhaled through his nose. He knew this question would come back to him in one way or another.

"She said she wanted to look like a crocus. Be grateful I picked the color of the petals and not the stem," he said ironically. "She's not my daughter. I found her in an orchard climbing trees like a feral animal, and I am trying to find her relatives to bring her home."

The bartender looked at the girl while still polishing the glass he was holding.

"What's her name?" He finally asked.

"She said she's named Omikraya," Lusstoni responded.

"And my mom always calls me Little Star!" The girl added.

"It doesn't sound like she could be from here," the bartender said and put the glass under the counter. "How old are you, Omikraya?"

"I'm ten!" She exclaimed happily.

"You're already ten?" Lusstoni asked without hiding his surprise.

"I will be ten at some point..." she responded. "I am basically an adult now!"

Lusstoni tried not to explode once more. If Omikraya were a little better at counting, he would have known that she was actually nine years and seven months old; alas, it wasn't meant to be.

The bartender shook his head.

"She doesn't sound or look like anyone from here," he said. "I don't think it's wise for you to keep scouring this place."

"But maybe—" Lusstoni tried to say something, but the man behind the counter interrupted him.

"Listen up, pretty boy. This town is not for the likes of you," he said harshly. "You come here in your fancy aircraft and strut around in your expensive clothes like you own the place, but here the people of the streets are in charge. There's a child with you. Be responsible and leave while you still have a vehicle to fly away in."

Lusstoni looked around and noticed that all the thugs were now glancing at him with thirsty looks on their faces.

"And if you'll take my advice," the bartender continued, "give her a bath and some fresh clothes. She looks like she has been living among the pigs. Her parents are going to kill you if you present her like this."

"Fine, but can I order something before we leave?" Lusstoni asked.

The bartender shook his head.

"You have a child with you. All I can give you is water until you return alone."

"But why? I would pay."

"Because I have principles unlike those self-righteous people in Norath. Now leave," the bartender said and pointed at the door. He was clearly done with this conversation.

Lusstoni still could feel the glances on him as they walked out of the bar. He was annoyed, although he wasn't sure if it was because he couldn't get a drink or because he still wasn't any closer to finding Omikraya's family. The longer it takes, the more time she will spend around and prevent him from enjoying his journey.

The man approached the nearest stall with clothes to buy her a new outfit.

"May I have a pretty dress?" Omikraya asked as she tightened her sweaty grasp around his hand.

"You may have what I buy for you," he responded and picked a simple dress, wooden shoes, and fresh underwear.

The vendor silently handed him the goods and put the payment in a box under the stall.

Omikraya was close to having a meltdown, but Lusstoni hanged the dress on his golden arm and led her back into the airship.

The man entered the airship and tossed the purchase on the side. He then grabbed a large metal bowl.

Lusstoni dropped it to the ground with a loud noise and fetched a bucket to go to the nearest well.

"What are you doing?" Omikraya asked as she skipped behind him.

"I'm going to give you a bath," he said.

He attached his bucket to the rope of the well and lowered it. The man pulled it back up and inspected the water.

_It's clean enough_, he thought, and carried the bucket in front of his airship. He emptied it into the large bowl and repeated the process a couple of times until it was filled.

Lusstoni brought her a brick of gray soap and pointed at the vessel.

"Take off these rugs you are wearing and get in the tub," he said.

Omikraya removed her old dirty clothes and sat in the tub in only her underwear.

"It's cold!" She protested.

"I don't care," he responded and handed her the soap. "Here, wash your hair."

The girl curled up in nearly a fetal position and started rubbing the back of her head with the gray soap.

"Not like this," Lusstoni shouted. "Put some water on your hair first...and stop crouching forward; you will get everything in your eyes! Don't you know how to wash your own head? You are too old to make these mistakes."

"I never did it alone," she complained as the water she just poured on her hair started getting in her eyes.

Lusstoni sighed impatiently and knelt behind her, grabbing her head with both his hands. The metallic parts of his prosthetic arm were catching rogue strands of her hair.

"Ouch, why do you have to wear this glove for it?" She asked.

"I...need it," he responded and tried to release her trapped hair. He never thought about it; when it happened to him, he just embraced it, thinking he deserved the pain for all he had done. He never considered that not everyone was disturbed this way.

After what felt like an eternity, Lusstoni finally managed to clean her hair. He brought her a towel, dried her head with it, and then wrapped it around her body.

"Go back to the airship," he said as he poured the water on the grass. For a second he started wondering if the soap would damage it. He then shrugged and thought, _That's not my problem._

Lusstoni lingered outside for a moment. He needed a while for himself.

The man took a cigarette out, lit it, and began smoking. He took a deep puff and exhaled slowly.

_Why did Caermirella want it so much?_ He thought to himself. _Children are such feeble creatures who can't be left unsupervised for five minutes without a crisis. We could have done so much and traveled so far. Why did she choose to dedicate her life to someone else?_

Lusstoni sighed and extinguished the rest of his cigarette. He threw it behind himself and headed into his airship.

He looked around.

_Why is it so quiet here?_ He thought. _Where is she?_

"Omikraya?" He asked and checked the spots where she used to hide. She wasn't there.

Lusstoni scratched his head cluelessly. He finally opened the door to the bedchamber and stood in the doorframe, mortified.

Omikraya was sitting on the floor in her wet underwear and painting something on a piece of paper with the machine grease he stored behind his dresser. Her hands were elbow-deep in the dark orange substance, which also covered her face and thighs. The floor was wet and there was grease everywhere.

"OMIKRAYA, I'VE JUST BATHED YOU!!!" He shouted.

The girl looked up, visibly not knowing why he was yelling.

"I know!" She said. "I made you a gift as thanks!"

Lusstoni looked at her hands. She was holding a portrait of a woman in one and the prized picture of Caermirella from his wall in the other.

He swiftly snatched it from her greasy fingers.

"How did you unpin it?" He shouted.

"It was easy," she said. "I just put a box in there to reach it!"

Lusstoni tried to breathe deeply to calm himself down.

"She's pretty. Is it your wife?" The girl asked.

"She could be," he responded. "Now she is my brother's wife. Her name is Caermirella."

Omikraya looked at him in confusion.

"Why do you have a photo of your brother's wife here?" She asked.

"It's a long story," he said. "She used to be with me."

"I don't understand," she admitted. "She doesn't like you anymore? Is that why she chose your brother?"

"I guess you could say that," he responded.

"Why do you keep pictures of people who no longer like you?" She asked. "That's weird."

"You are weird," he cut short as he cleaned the photo of Caermirella with a napkin and put it in the hidden pocket of his coat. "Sit in the corridor and wait for me to clean this place before I give you another bath. Do **not** go into the cockpit."

He angrily cleaned the bedchamber. The machine grease was hard to remove, but he finally managed.

Lusstoni picked up the portrait Omikraya made for him. He wanted to tear it into pieces, but he just put it into one of his junk drawers.

The man finished cleaning and wiped his hands with a napkin. He walked out of the bedchamber and looked towards the cockpit. He saw Omikraya playing in there.

_Why doesn't it surprise me?_ He thought to himself.

Lusstoni approached her and said, "Get out; you are getting another bath."

"But I don't want to!"

"I am not asking you if you want to; you are not allowed to be here if you are getting my airship wet and dirty," he stated firmly.

Omikraya sadly walked out of the cockpit and went in front of the airship. Lusstoni gave her another bath; he then took a moment to wash all the cloth she stained before he retreated.

He brought the wet items back in and hanged them under the ceiling of his deck.

"Get dressed and don't go anywhere near my grease, understood?"

Omikraya grumpily took her new clothes and locked herself in the bedchamber to put them on.

The girl went out with a crabby face. She looked much different when she was clean.

"I said I wanted a pretty dress," she said.

"It is pretty; you just have no taste," he responded.

The dress was fairly simple. The white ruffled top flowed smoothly into the light brown bottom, tied with a pale purple ribbon at the belly.

"Do I have to wear these shoes?" She asked miserably.

Lusstoni confirmed.

"But they are so loud!" She exclaimed.

"Exactly," he said. "You won't sneak up on me anymore. The shoes have to stay if you want to be here."

Omikraya crossed her arms grumpily.

"Toni?" She finally said. "Can you tie my hair into two braids?"

Lusstoni wanted to decline. However, he still had some faint memories of helping Caermirella with her hair. The man decided to try. He nodded and went into the bedchamber to find some ribbons and a comb.

Omikraya sat on a small stool in the main chamber of the deck. She looked at him and grimaced.

"Can you take the glove off now?"

"No," he responded and knelt behind her.

There was no need for him to wash her hair again, so it was almost dry. The man was relieved that he didn't need to wait any longer.

Lusstoni clumsily began combing her hair. He tried not to catch any strands in his prosthetic arm this time. The texture of her hair felt strange under his fingers. It was soft and silky, unlike his own, which was dry and brittle.

Omikraya tried to look around, but he kept pushing her head back in position.

"I can't do anything if you keep moving. Sit still," he said.

After a while he remembered how to do it, and he created two clumsy braids. They could have been better had somebody else made them, but he is all she had, so she had to accept them.

Lusstoni tied two blue ribbons on the bottom of the braids and breathed out in relief.

"Done," he said. "Now go play with something and I will return to the cockpit. I got to move the airship away from here."

"But I want to play with you!"

Lusstoni shook his head.

"I will not play with you," he said.

The girl looked at the ground sadly. Lusstoni promptly ignored it and went into the bedchamber to place his comb back into the junk drawer. As he opened it, he saw his old wooden spinning top.

_Maybe this will buy me some time_, he thought.

Omikraya heard him coming and looked up.

"What is this?" She asked, pointing at the item he was holding.

"It's my old toy," he answered. "You may have it if you play quietly."

Omikraya squeaked happily and grabbed the spinning top. She then sat on the floor and put it on the ground.

"What does it do?" She asked.

Lusstoni didn't think he would ever meet a child who doesn't know how to play with it.

"You got to make it spin, like this," he said and showed her how to do it.

Omikraya grasped it quickly, and she began playing on her own.

"I thought it would take her more time," he said to himself and returned to his cockpit.

The man sat down heavily in his pilot chair and put the goggles back on his face. He checked if everything was still set as it should be. Surprisingly, Omikraya didn't flip any switches.

The airship lifted from the ground slowly. Lusstoni set the course to the next town he could bring her to. He was already tired of taking care of her, and their adventure had just began.

The man hoped he could be alone soon.

Lusstoni took the picture of Caermirella out of his hidden pocket and rubbed it with his thumb.

"I am not weird, right?" He said under his breath. "The child is because she has never been in love."

Deep inside he knew she was right, but he would never admit it. He wanted to believe that if he could prove he had changed, Caermirella would return to him.

Lusstoni was too comfortable in the embrace of delusion to accept the truth.
