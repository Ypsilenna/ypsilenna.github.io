Lusstoni got up unusually early for him. Omikraya was still asleep, so he decided to take his tools and begin searching for the things they needed to deactivate the core.

He left the airship and took a deep breath. It was early morning, and the sky was the color of a blushing peach. The man never paid attention to the world around him until he didn't have anything else left to do.

Lusstoni clumsily climbed on top of the rocks by the end of the south-eastern beach. He looked into the distance and attempted to figure out where the Cursed King should be at the moment based on Ivi's explanation. The beast was heading to the western village, just as he expected.

The man set off toward the lake with a hammer in his hand. He started walking along the shore in his search for the purple crystals when suddenly the mysterious nymph appeared once more above the surface of the still water.

"You figured out how to leave the island, I assume," she said. "Where is the princess?"

Lusstoni straightened his back as if he were just scolded by his teacher and looked at the woman.

"I did," he answered. "Omikraya is asleep on the deck of my airship. She is safe."

"Good," the nymph said and smiled very softly. It seemed like the girl's well-being brought her satisfaction.

"Where are the other maidens?" Lusstoni asked.

"Gone," she responded. "I am the last custodian of Crescent Moon Isle."

Lusstoni frowned.

"Then there is no hope that we could find her mother?"

The nymphs shook her head.

"I told you she gave everything to save her," she said.

"I just don't know how to tell her about it," Lusstoni sighed.

The man's grip on the handle of his hammer tightened. He didn't want to be the bearer of bad news, but he will have to tell her sooner or later.

"I met someone on this island who told me how to find and deactivate the core," he continued. What do you know about the hermit in the western village?"

"His story is not mine to tell," she said.

"The scrolls said that the core maintains the magic on this island. What would happen to you if it's weakened too severely or if it's completely destroyed?"

"That's a mortal misconception," the nymph said. "Divine magic is the fabric of this universe, even if you do not see it. The core allows humans to wield it, but it doesn't maintain anything other than the barrier above the island. If the artifact ceases to work, I will simply restore this island to what it once was and return to the stars where I belong. Such is the will of my masters, the primal gods."

Lusstoni's attention was drawn by a fine-looking purple crystal. He thought it would be suitable for the task, but he didn't want to end the conversation just yet.

"Why would the goddess gift King Callum an evil artifact?" he asked. "Seems rather strange that her majesty would curse her most devoted follower this way."

The nymph shook her head.

"The core is neither good nor evil," she responded. "The king proved unworthy of wielding its power. He is the cause of this curse."

Lusstoni knelt in front of the crystal and gently poked around it with a hammer.

"Such misjudgment seems unfitting for a literal god," he said. "Shouldn't she know there was something in him that would cause this mess?"

The woman sighed.

"I think she simply wanted to believe," she said. "Each primal god has their own strengths and limitations, which is why they create their divine servants to make up for it. Goddess Omikraya is fond of humans; she wants to find good in them. Sometimes perhaps too much. Not to mention, her condition doesn't make it easier for her to properly judge the mortals who worship her."

"That was an expensive mistake to make," Lusstoni commented and accidentally broke the crystal he was trying to extract. He quickly found another one, but it didn't stop him from whispering some profanities under his breath.

"Perhaps," the nymph said, "but we cannot afford to dwell on it anymore. The girl seems to be all that remains of this once great kingdom. We all had to pay to protect her, and I ask you once more on behalf of all those who fell on Crescent Moon Isle to keep her safe. You don't have to let her stay with you, but please, don't make her pay for this mistake as well. She still has a bright future ahead of her."

"I won't," Lusstoni responded while attempting to harvest another crystal. The object seemed incredibly slippery, and he had to be really careful not to break another one. "I am just displeased that I got to do it in the first place. Her home should be safe for her."

"I understand, but we don't have any choice," the nymph spread her arms. "Still, I am glad she ended up with you."

Lusstoni looked at her with a surprise in his eyes, nearly breaking another crystal. Luckily he stopped his hammer just in time.

"Why would you say that?" he asked. "I am just a bum with a flying machine who breaks more than he fixes. Just look at the mess I am making here!"

The nymph glanced at the broken crystals and shook her head.

"You judge yourself too harshly," she said. "You are more than you realize; never forget that."

The woman disappeared into thin air, leaving only fog behind.

"Nonsense," Lusstoni muttered. He was not used to being praised, and he didn't think her words were genuine.

Once he was no longer distracted, he finally managed to harvest a good crystal without breaking it. He gently put the item into his toolbox and went back to his airship.

Omikraya was still asleep, so he decided to continue with his plan. Once she woke up, she saw him mixing some powders into small spherical shells.

"Toni, what are you doing?" she asked and rubbed her eye.

"I'm making explosives," he responded.

"Is that safe?"

"You're asking a one-armed man," he stated. "No, it's not, but I need something that will make some noise while I cut a hole in the vault under the castle. The alternative would be wasting my time looking for a key, and I do not want that."

"What are you going to do with them?"

"I want to bury them on the western beach," he explained. "Do you see this remote? I want to connect the explosives to it so that I can activate them remotely."

"Woah, you are so smart!" Omikraya squeaked with excitation.

"No, this is the kind of thing I was doing during school," he said. "One day they had to evacuate everyone because I put one of these in the bathroom. Good times," he laughed and resumed working on his explosives.

"Why did you do that?" Omikraya was confused.

"I thought it would be funny," he answered. "These explosives aren't very strong; they just make a lot of noise. I thought it would be hilarious to detonate it while some poor soul sits down to make them think the noise came out of their rear end."

"Was it funny, then?"

"It would be if it didn't get me expelled and lose a year," he said. "My parents prevented me from making explosives again, so I decided to use my talents in a different way. I made some smoke bombs and sneaked into the girls' room during a school trip. I was planning to use my creation to escape had they discovered me."

"Did it work?"

"No, I mixed the rooms and accidentally invaded the space of the nurse who came with us," he said. "I dropped one of the smoke bombs, and they had to evacuate the entire building once more thinking it was on fire. My teacher was holding my hand all the way back to my city, and then my parents confiscated my tinkering set."

Omikraya scratched her head.

"Why did you want to go there anyway?" she asked.

Lusstoni shrugged.

"I just wanted to see what the girls were doing," he said. "They never wanted to be friends with me, and I was curious."

The man carefully put his explosives into a small box and fetched two small shovels from the storage space next to the bedchamber.

"You can help me dig if you want to," he said.

"Hooray!" Omikraya shouted and grabbed one of the shovels. "I will dig the deepest hole! May I also make a sand castle?"

"If we get enough time," Lusstoni responded. "We should do it quickly to ensure your daddy won't catch us there, though."

"I'll be the fastest!" Omikraya said and saluted.

They left the airship and headed toward the other beach. Lusstoni felt slightly bad for bringing the girl with him right after the nymph complimented him, but she should have known better than to say good things about Lusstonis such as himself.

The western beach was empty, and the sounds of the Cursed King were so distant that Lusstoni was sure he wouldn't bother them, but he said nothing. Omikraya would have turned it into a family beach day, and they didn't have **that** much time.

In the heat of passion, the girl dug up many more holes than he needed. She found some seashells and some unknown brown rocks under the pale sand.

Omikraya just wouldn't stop talking about them.

Lusstoni gently placed explosives in the ground and covered them with a thin layer of sand. He got up and grabbed his box.

"Let's go," he said. "I got to fetch my metal cutter."

Lusstoni slowly moved toward their camp. Omikraya happily skipped behind him.

"Toni, do you think we will find my mom in the secret chamber?"

The man froze for a moment.

"Maybe," he finally answered and resumed walking without maintaining eye contact with her.

"I think she will like you," she said. "Maybe you could become her f—"

"Omikraya, stop talking," Lusstoni interrupted her. The lump he felt in his throat kept getting bigger.

The girl looked at the ground sadly and went quiet.

They returned to the airship. Lusstoni left some of his items in there and grabbed the crystal, the metal cutter, and a small energy generator.

Right before they headed back out, Lusstoni stopped in the door and looked back at his things. He wanted to get a good sight of what served as his home for what felt like weeks. The man felt like he won't ever see this place again.

A sudden thought weighted on his heart. He probably will never see Caermirella either.

Before reaching his destination, Lusstoni set up some traps on the road to the castle. He handed Omikraya a bunch of large leaves and told her to use them to cover up their footsteps behind them. He had to make sure King Callum would not follow them too soon.

Lusstoni sighed, and they headed toward the castle. He found the hidden crevice and put his tools down on the stone floor.

"Toni, what is this?" Omikraya asked and pointed at one of his devices.

"It's a portable energy generator," he said. "I need it to fuel the metal cutter."

"How does it work?"

The generator looked like a blue cylinder. Omikraya had never seen anything like this.

Lusstoni lit up a match and opened the hidden compartment on the side.

"There is some water inside," he said. "When it gets hot, it turns into steam that helps generate energy."

"Oh," she mumbled. "I don't understand."

The man ignited the reactive compound inside and closed the compartment. The water flashed into steam, which began spinning something inside the machine with a low hum.

Omikraya was both fascinated and terrified.

Lusstoni plugged his cutter into the generator and put his goggles on his eyes.

"Step aside, it's going to be loud," he said.

The man took one more glance behind them. The Cursed King was nowhere to be found.

Lusstoni pressed the button on his remote. The explosives started going off one by one, spaced enough to give him time to create a way in.

Omikraya was terrified. She hid herself in the bushes and covered her ears with both hands. She had never heard so much noise at once.

The girl closed her eyes and waited. She suddenly felt Lusstoni's touch on her shoulder.

"What are you doing there?"

The girl opened her eyes and saw Lusstoni standing above her. The noise was gone.

"Are you done?" she asked.

"Yes, it was quite easy," he responded. "I can't believe it worked. Ha, a five hundred-year-old spell versus my new metal cutter. Human technology is superior to magic."

Without wasting a moment, he grabbed his tools and entered the secret vault through the hole. He expected the inside to be dark, but to his surprise it was a beautiful natural cave with glowing crystals sticking out from every corner. There was what appeared to be a small cave lake with a little isle in the middle.

Lusstoni glanced at the center of the water and saw a massive shiny pearl resting on a pedestal.

"That must be the core," he said. "Let's get this over with."

They walked down the path to reach the artifact. Soft sun rays peaked from a small hole on the ceiling. The secret vault looked perfect in every way.

"Wait for me here; I will go through the water," he said and headed toward the core.

Omikraya observed him hauling his beloved tools across the shallow water. She sat down on a rock and waved her legs.

Lusstoni placed the cutter and the generator on the ground and approached the artifact. The core looked like a giant pearl. Its surface glistened with many beautiful colors.

The man took a crystal out of his pocket and glanced at it.

"I hope it works," he said quietly, almost as if he didn't want anyone to hear it.

Lusstoni grabbed the crystal with his prosthetic arm to avoid touching the magic with his bare skin. As soon as the object found itself in proximity to the artifact, the core began emitting a strange aura. The purple amorphous ooze bounced off it and fluctuated, desperately trying to move itself toward the crystal.

"Just like magnets," he said and pressed the object against the artifact, attempting to collect the magic it bled.

"Toni!!!" Omikraya shouted desperately.

At first he didn't know what happened. The man lifted his eyes up and saw the girl climbing the rocks against the wall in a hurry. Trying to figure out what she was fleeing from, he looked around and saw the Cursed King slowly approaching her.

The sight spooked him so much that he nearly dropped the crystal. Lusstoni twitched and accidentally kicked the cable connecting his metal cutter and the power source. He suddenly realized that he didn't turn off the portable generator.

The man tried to focus, but the vision of destroying his devices was almost as scary as the monster before him.

King Callum tried to catch Omikraya, but she climbed into a hole too small and high for him to reach. Suddenly his eyes turned to Lusstoni. He wanted to run toward the man and attack; however, the moment he touched the water, an unpleasant electric shock went through his body.

Not able to cross, the Cursed King decided to resort to more diplomatic measures. After all, any sudden move could put his precious artifact in danger.

"So you brought back my daughter, yet you attempt to drain the core," he said. "I don't think you realize the power within it. Now that I can finally have what I wanted, I could finally shift her into this superior form and fulfill our destiny. Why don't you stay with us...forever?"

The voice of the beast was strangely distinguished; Lusstoni never would have guessed it didn't belong to a human... if King Callum could still be considered one.

The man said nothing. He noticed the myriad of red glowing stripes that Ivi told him about. It was clear that physically confronting him made no sense.

"And if the two of you refuse the core, you will end up dead just like her mother. There is no way out from Crescent Moon Isle," the beast continued. "I saw you before...all that suffering, all that pain...all that ails you could be healed with the magic of the core if you stop draining it. Your leg could be healthy once more."

With that one word, Lusstoni realized that he never wandered around the island without his poncho. _Leg_. The king probably didn't realize he was missing an arm. The man decided to use that fact to his advantage.

"Just put your hand on the core, and your life will be as if your past never happened."

"Toni, don't do that!" Omikraya shouted, further pushing herself against the wall in fear.

The eyes of the Cursed King bounced between Lusstoni and the terrified princess.

"Don't listen to her; she is but a child," the beast hissed.

Lusstoni nodded and sneakily put his hand on the core.

Omikraya began screeching like a damned soul.

King Callum smiled in a sinister way, revealing two rows of white pointy teeth.

"Good choice," he said. "Can you feel the power coursing through your veins?"

"I have never been happier..." Lusstoni said as he gently lifted the core from the pedestal. "...that I lost my arm eight years ago."

Straining the words through his teeth, the man threw the artifact to the ground with the power of all the feelings he repressed until that day, shattering the core violently into a thousand pieces.

In that very second, King Callum understood what had transpired and desperately threw himself toward Lusstoni, jumping above the water.

The man thought he couldn't dodge the attack on time, yet a rusty knife cut the air in front of him and pierced the beast's body, causing the Cursed King to fall to the ground.

Lusstoni had no time to inquire what happened. For the first time in years, he felt that somebody relied on him, and he wanted to make sure that this monster wouldn't harm anyone else.

King Callum's body hit the ground with a loud thump, yet he was still alive.

"What have you done?" he said with a big effort. "The core could have healed you. It could have healed everyone. You have doomed us."

Lusstoni knelt above him while clenching the fingers of his healthy hand on the handle of the hammer he brought with him.

"Say what you want, but this island doesn't seem healed to me," he said. "I got to admit, though, it must feel terrible to have your plans foiled by a miserable one-armed alcoholic."

King Callum raised his eyes as if he wanted to say something when suddenly his skin went gray and his body turned to ash.

Lusstoni turned around and disabled the generator. He sighed with an effort and crossed the shallow water to reunite with Omikraya.

The girl climbed down, barely keeping her shaking hands on the stones. She looked at Lusstoni, and her blue eyes filled with tears.

"I'm sorry that I evaporated your father," he said in a dry tone.

"I thought you would let him take me," she blurted out through tears.

"I would never do that," he responded the same way as when he spoke about his childhood.

"And I have just realized that I will never see my mom again," she continued crying.

Lusstoni looked at her. As much as he didn't want to admit it, he felt incredibly sorry for her. His lips bent slightly. The man didn't know what to say to make the situation better. All he knew was what he never got in situations like these. If anything in his life ever came close to _this_.

Omikraya tried to wipe her tears, but each time she did so, her face was only getting increasingly wet.

Lusstoni knelt in front of her and gave her a hug. This time it was out of his free will.

The girl buried her face in his clothes.

"I don't have anyone anymore," she wept. "I will just stay here alone as you leave me behind."

"I will take you to Norath with me," Lusstoni said, trying to comfort her. He wanted to tell her something more while a familiar voice interrupted him.

"Where is the king?" Ivi asked as he appeared in the chamber. "I could still see him when I threw the knife. I want him to answer for his crimes."

Lusstoni stood up and looked at the hermit.

"I'm sorry," he said. "I thought destroying the artifact would only weaken him, but he turned into a pile of ash, I'm afraid."

Ivi looked at the remains on the ground and his rusty knife lying next to them.

"Good," he said and turned around. "It's the fate he deserves for what he had done to my brothers. I will await you at the entrance."

The hermit walked away.

Lusstoni was about to collect his tools from the ground when suddenly a beam of warm white light brightened the entire chamber. Little reflections from crystals danced on its walls.

The man narrowed his eyes.

In front of him stood a young woman with her eyes closed. Her long silver hair hovered around her as if she were suspended in water. She was wearing a white flowy dress that looked like sea foam.

She spread her arms toward Lusstoni.

"You cleansed my island," she said. "This is no small feat, for which I want to reward you. I am Goddess Omikraya, and you may ask me for whatever your heart desires."

Lusstoni picked up his tools and headed toward the exit.

"No, thanks," he said.

"No? Are you certain?" the goddess asked. "My magic could grant anything you have ever dreamt of."

"After what I witnessed on this island I never wish to see magic again," he responded without slowing down. "Princess, we're going."

"A mortal who refuses a reward?" Goddess Omikraya said to herself, "How peculiar."

She raised her face up and disappeared as quickly as she arrived, leaving only shimmering stardust behind.

Omikraya clumsily followed Lusstoni. She already stopped crying, but her face still looked like a ripe tomato.

"Toni, why didn't you accept the gift of the goddess?" she asked. "Her magic could make you healthy again. Maybe you could have Caermirella back—"

"Magic is not a solution," he erupted a little harder than he thought he would. "That's why in Norath we bow to no gods. These powers are not meant for us, and they do more harm than good. If it wasn't for magic, I wouldn't be here, and you still would have your family. Don't ask me to accept that _gift_ and be happy about it."

The girl looked down and didn't respond.

"Not to mention, she could see it all along and did nothing to stop the corruption," he said under his breath.

The two walked out of the secret chamber. The sun slightly blinded Lusstoni.

The man put his tools on the ground.

Ivi approached him from behind a wall. Lusstoni wouldn't admit how happy he was to see the hermit again.

"How did you destroy the core without touching it?" Ivi asked without hiding his curiosity.

"He only has one arm," Omikraya said gloomily.

"What?" Ivi was confused. "I can see two."

"One of them is fake," Lusstoni explained without betraying any emotions. "They made it for me in Norath after the...accident."

Ivi enthusiastically grabbed the prosthetic with both hands.

"Impossible!" he shouted. "It moves just like a real one. Marvelous!"

"Do you mind?" Lusstoni said as he pulled his mechanical arm back.

"Oh, I'm sorry!" Ivi apologized. "This is the first time I see one. It's rather strange that it was enough to prevent the spread of magic."

"I never would have thought that the things I hate the most about myself would be what would be the most needed," Lusstoni admitted.

The man sighed and sat down on the ground to rest. He watched Ivi and Omikraya talk. He couldn't help but notice how similar they looked. Could it be that simply everyone in this kingdom looked alike? Perhaps.

Lusstoni finally asked, "Now that the king is no more, why wouldn't you stay here with her?"

Ivi glanced at him, visibly surprised by the question. After a moment of brief silence, he finally shook his head and said, "She can't go where I'm going. Not to mention, she should stay with someone she already knows."

"But I can't even take care of myself. I am terrible," Lusstoni shouted.

Ivi made an unfazed expression.

"You say that, but here she is, clean, safe, and well fed," he said. "Princess, do you think he's right?"

Omikraya looked at them both, put her index finger on her chin, and said, "No. Toni is the greatest."

Lusstoni felt a little wrong. He spent his entire life running from responsibilities. How could they finally catch up with him?

A moment of silence fell. The man finally sighed and asked, "Could you leave me alone for a moment? I need to collect my thoughts."

Ivi nodded and took Omikraya down the road toward one of the villages.

Lusstoni grabbed the pack of cigarettes from the inner pocket of his coat. He gently opened it and looked inside.

"Only two," he complained as he grabbed one of the cigarettes.

The man lit up one of the cigarettes and greedily took a drag on it until he started coughing.

"This is not good for me," he said without stopping.

His eyes followed the silhouettes of the people he knew.

"Old man, you will have to seek help if we are about to be needed," he said to himself as if he were scolding a stranger.

Lusstoni took a deep breath and stretched his back. Without the magic barrier, the island seemed almost idyllic. He wondered what would happen if he did stay there.

The man extinguished his cigarette and grabbed his head.

The bad memories never loosened their grip on him. He couldn't remove Caermirella's face out of his mind. A wave of conflicting feelings hit him once more. He no longer knew what he really wanted.

Something touched his hand, pulling him out of the pit of despair. He lifted his palm to his face. It was a beautiful blue butterfly. Its iridescent wings looked like the surface of a soap bubble.

Lusstoni never slowed down to watch the little lives, but here it was, a beautiful little insect watching him instead.

"What are you doing here?" he asked. "You have all these flowers around and yet you choose to sit on a miserable old man?"

The butterfly turned around and then looked at him again.

"Go and sit on something beautiful," Lusstoni said as he tried to shake the insect off his hand.

The butterfly started flying around him and then sat on his face. Lusstoni tried to scare it off, but the insect refused to leave him alone. It walked up to his top hat and began cleaning its eyes.

"You have no taste," Lusstoni sighed.
